## 1. `zheng2023judging`

- **Record:** NeurIPS proceedings page
  <https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/91f18a1287b398d378ef22505bf41832-Abstract-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.html>
  (vol. 36, pp. 46595–46623, DOI 10.52202/075280-2020; DOI confirmed in Crossref
  <https://api.crossref.org/works/10.52202/075280-2020>). Full author names from arXiv
  <https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685> (v4; comment "NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks Track").
- **Finding:** The paper names position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases of LLM judges, and reports that GPT-4 agrees with human experts on MT-Bench at 85 % (non-tie votes), higher than the 81 % agreement among humans.
- **Quotes** (NeurIPS PDF, §3.3 "Limitations of LLM-as-a-Judge", pp. 4–5; §4.2):
  - "Position bias is when an LLM exhibits a propensity to favor certain positions over others."
  - "Verbosity bias is when an LLM judge favors longer, verbose responses, even if they are not as clear, high-quality, or accurate as shorter alternatives."
  - "We adopt the term “self-enhancement bias” from social cognition literature [4] to describe the effect that LLM judges may favor the answers generated by themselves."
  - "The agreement under setup S2 (w/o tie) between GPT-4 and humans reaches 85%, which is even higher than the agreement among humans (81%)."
  - Abstract: "strong LLM judges like GPT-4 can match both controlled and crowdsourced human preferences well, achieving over 80% agreement, the same level of agreement between humans."
- **⚠ Caveat for the report:** their self-enhancement evidence is inconclusive by their own account: "Due to limited data and small differences, our study cannot determine whether the models exhibit a self-enhancement bias." Cite Panickssery et al. for self-preference evidence, not Zheng et al.
- Other numbers on the same pages if needed: GPT-4 position consistency 65.0 % (default prompt, Table 2); "repetitive list" verbosity-attack failure rate Claude-v1 91.3 %, GPT-3.5 91.3 %, GPT-4 8.7 % (Table 3).

## 2. `liu2023geval`

- **Record:** ACL Anthology <https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.153/> (BibTeX at
  <https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.153.bib>): EMNLP 2023, pp. 2511–2522,
  DOI 10.18653/v1/2023.emnlp-main.153. arXiv <https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634> (v3).
  The Anthology renders the title as "{G}pt-4"; the paper's own title page reads "GPT-4", which the .bib uses.
- **Finding:** G-Eval has the LLM generate chain-of-thought evaluation steps and fill in a scoring form, and the authors find that G-Eval-4 always scored GPT-3.5 summaries higher than human-written ones, even when human judges preferred the human summaries.
- **Quotes:**
  - Abstract: "a framework of using large language models with chain-of-thoughts (CoT) and a form-filling paradigm, to assess the quality of NLG outputs."
  - §1: "By only feeding the Task Introduction and the Evaluation Criteria as a prompt, we ask LLMs to generate a CoT of detailed Evaluation Steps. Then we use the prompt along with the generated CoT to evaluate the NLG outputs. The evaluator output is formatted as a form."
  - §4 (Analysis), p. 2516: "G-EVAL-4 always gives higher scores to GPT-3.5 summaries than human-written summaries, even when human judges prefer human-written summaries."
  - Abstract: "highlight the potential issue of LLM-based evaluators having a bias towards the LLM-generated texts."
- Also from the abstract: Spearman 0.514 with humans on summarization (GPT-4 backbone).

## 3. `ribeiro2020beyond`

- **Record:** ACL Anthology <https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.442/> (BibTeX
  <https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.442.bib>): ACL 2020, pp. 4902–4912,
  DOI 10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.442.
- **Finding:** CheckList tests NLP models with three test types: Minimum Functionality tests (simple targeted cases), Invariance tests (label-preserving perturbations that must not change the output), and Directional Expectation tests (perturbations whose effect on the label is known).
- **Quotes** (§2.2 "Test Types"):
  - "A Minimum Functionality test (MFT), inspired by unit tests in software engineering, is a collection of simple examples (and labels) to check a behavior within a capability."
  - "An Invariance test (INV) is when we apply label-preserving perturbations to inputs and expect the model prediction to remain the same."
  - "A Directional Expectation test (DIR) is similar, except that the label is expected to change in a certain way."

## 4. `doddapaneni2024finding`

- **Record:** ACL Anthology <https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.911/> (BibTeX
  <https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.911.bib>): EMNLP 2024, pp. 16279–16309,
  DOI 10.18653/v1/2024.emnlp-main.911. arXiv <https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.13439> (v2, comment "EMNLP 2024"). Venue confirmed: **EMNLP 2024 main conference**.
- **Finding:** Using 2,400 answers perturbed with targeted quality drops across 22 categories, they find five widely used evaluator LLMs failed to detect the drop in over 50 % of cases on average, with reference-based evaluation doing comparatively better than single-answer or pairwise scoring.
- **Quotes** (abstract):
  - "By introducing targeted perturbations in answers generated by LLMs, that clearly impact one of these key capabilities, we test whether an Evaluator LLM can detect these quality drops."
  - "Our findings reveal significant shortcomings in current Evaluator LLMs, which failed to identify quality drops in over 50% of cases on average. Single-answer and pairwise evaluations demonstrated notable limitations, whereas reference-based evaluations showed comparatively better performance."
  - "By creating a total of 2400 perturbed answers covering 22 perturbation categories, we conduct a comprehensive study using different evaluation strategies on five prominent LLMs commonly used as evaluators in the literature."

## 5. `panickssery2024llm`

  <https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/7f1f0218e45f5414c79c0679633e47bc-Abstract-Conference.html>

  - "out of the box, LLMs such as GPT-4 and Llama 2 have non-trivial accuracy at distinguishing themselves from other LLMs and humans. By fine-tuning LLMs, we discover a linear correlation between self-recognition capability and the strength of self-preference bias"

## 6. `dror2018hitchhikers`

- **Record:** ACL Anthology <https://aclanthology.org/P18-1128/> (BibTeX

  - §3.2 "Prominent Significance Tests" (on the Wilcoxon signed-rank test): "The test is actually applicable for most NLP setups and it has been used widely"

## 8a. `patil2024gorilla`

- **Record:** NeurIPS proceedings
  <https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/e4c61f578ff07830f5c37378dd3ecb0d-Abstract-Conference.html>
  (vol. 37, pp. 126544–126565, DOI 10.52202/079017-4020, confirmed in Crossref).
  arXiv <https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15334>. Venue confirmed: **NeurIPS 2024**.
- **Finding:** Gorilla introduces the APIBench benchmark and evaluates API calls by matching the abstract syntax tree (AST) of the generated call against the reference call, which measures both functional correctness and API hallucination.
- **Quotes** (NeurIPS PDF):
  - "developing an evaluation framework that uses AST sub-tree matching to check functional correctness."
  - "We present APIBench, a comprehensive benchmark of ∼ 1600 machine learning APIs, along with new AST-based evaluation metrics that precisely measure both functional correctness and API hallucination."
  - "checking if the AST of the candidate API call is a sub-tree of the reference API call reveals which API is being used in the dataset."

- **Records:** OpenAlex <https://api.openalex.org/works/W2121044470> (Holm, Scand. J. Statist.,
  vol. 6, pp. 65–70, 1979, with abstract); the full paper's JSTOR cover sheet (copy at

## 10. `wilcoxon1945individual`

- **Records:** Crossref <https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2307/3001968> (Biometrics Bulletin,
  vol. 1, no. 6, Dec. 1945; Crossref lists only first page 80). DOI resolves to JSTOR. Last page 83
  from the JSTOR cover sheet on two independent copies ("Biometrics Bulletin, Vol. 1, No. 6.
  (Dec., 1945), pp. 80-83."):
  <https://sci2s.ugr.es/keel/pdf/algorithm/articulo/wilcoxon1945.pdf> (downloaded and read) and

  (seen through search results).
- **Finding:** Wilcoxon proposes replacing the observed data, or paired differences, with rank numbers to get a quick approximate significance test, the basis of the signed-rank test for paired comparisons.
- **Quotes** (p. 80, read from the rendered page of the sci2s copy):
  - "The object of the present paper is to indicate the possibility of using ranking methods, that is, methods in which scores 1, 2, 3, . . . n are substituted for the actual numerical data, in order to obtain a rapid approximate idea of the significance of the differences in experiments of this kind."
  - Paired comparisons: "If we substitute rank numbers for these differences, we arrive at the series … The sum of the negative rank numbers is −24."

## 11. `cohen1968weighted`

- **Records:** Crossref (APA deposit) <https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1037/h0026256>
  (Psychological Bulletin, vol. 70, no. 4, pp. 213–220, 1968); PubMed / MEDLINE PMID 19673146

- **⚠ Title discrepancy:** APA's Crossref deposit reads "…Nominal scale agreement provision for

  The .bib uses the MEDLINE form, which is grammatical and is the commonly cited title. I could not

- **Quote** (abstract; the OpenAlex and PubMed-derived text stops mid-sentence after this):
  "A previously described coefficient of agreement for nominal scales, kappa, treats all disagreements equally. A generalization to weighted kappa (Kw) is presented. The Kw provides for the incorpation of ratio-scaled degrees of disagreement (or agreement) to each of the cells of the k * k table of joi[nt …]" ("incorpation" is as printed in the record.)

## 12. `kerby2014simple`

- **Records:** Crossref <https://api.crossref.org/works/10.2466/11.it.3.1> (Comprehensive
  Psychology, vol. 3, article 11.IT.3.1, Jan. 2014, Ammons Scientific / SAGE); full text from the
  SAGE article page <https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.2466/11.it.3.1>, retrieved through
  the web-search tool (direct fetch got a Cloudflare 403).
- **Finding:** Kerby shows that the rank-biserial correlation equals the proportion of favorable evidence minus the proportion of unfavorable evidence (r = f − u), where the evidence is pairs for the Mann–Whitney U test and rank sums for the Wilcoxon signed-rank test.
- **Quotes:**
  - Abstract: "The formula states that the correlation equals the simple difference between the proportion of favorable and unfavorable evidence; in symbols this is r = f – u. For the Mann-Whitney U, the evidence consists of pairs. For the signed-rank test, the evidence consists of rank sums."
  - Signed-rank section: "the formula states that the nonparametric correlation is the simple difference between the proportion of favorable and unfavorable evidence; in the case of the Wilcoxon signed-rank test, the evidence consists of rank sums."
