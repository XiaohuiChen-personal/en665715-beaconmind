# Adversarial review of AGENT-DESIGN.md v1 — 2026-09-27

Workflow `review-agent-design` (run `wf_3f296f0c-e5c`, Claude Opus 5.5) ran five independent
lenses: consistency/completeness, geometry/algorithm, safety/hardware, prompt-readiness, and a
hand-simulation on a 2.5 m arena with 4 beacons (one ID duplicated).

**Result:** 73 findings (5 critical, 37 major, 31 minor), with heavy overlap across lenses. The
main session re-checked each against the cited evidence, and **all were confirmed**. The
conditioning finding was reproduced numerically with `tools/l2_geometry.py`: σ_xy ≈ 0.30 m,
σ_θ = 8.0° for two beacons 1 m apart at 2 m. None was rejected.

**Disposition:** v1 was redesigned as v2 (same file). Grouped fixes:

######## LENS consistency-completeness — 16 findings

[1] (critical) D5 delivery model + D8 schema vs D6/D7 multi-step rules
  QUOTE: So belief and map need no read/write tools: the model's report *is* the state.
  PROBLEM: The model only receives OBSERVATION plus PREVIOUS_REPORT (the D8 object). That object cannot hold the history the D6/D7 rules depend on, so several rules cannot be carried out as written. Missing state: (a) "Two stalls in a row", "slip_suspected twice in a row" and "status: error twice in a row" (D6) need the step before the previous one, and PREVIOUS_REPORT has no odometry quality or observation status. (b) "no forward move on that heading" after a stall needs the stalled heading. (c) A scan is "12 × TURN_LEFT_30 over 12 steps" and a new place is "≥ 0.5 m from every earlier scan point", which needs scan progress and the list of scan points. (d) "two consecutive scans at new places add no new beacon" needs a counter. (e) A revisit is "≥ 1.5 m of travel, or ≥ 10 steps out of view", which needs cumulative distance and a last-seen step per beacon. (f) "confirmed after ≥ 2 observations from poses ≥ 0.25 m apart that agree within 0.30 m" needs the earlier observation poses and points, but a map entry keeps only a running mean and n_obs. (g) "re-observed and failed confirmation" (D7.8) is not in the D8 state enum `confirmed|tentative`. (h) The navigation budget of 60 needs the step the mission started. The first step (no PREVIOUS_REPORT) is not specified either.
  EVIDENCE: AGENT-DESIGN.md L114-121 (delivery model), L244-253 (D8 fields: step, mode, mission, pose, pose_source, associations, loop_closure, map{beacon,id,x_m,y_m,n_obs,state}, decision, move_result, note), L177-184 (D6), L212-232 (D7 steps 5-8), L149 (update_beacon_estimate returns only a running mean).
  FIX: Add an explicit `agent_state` block to D8 that the harness echoes back as PREVIOUS_REPORT. It should hold: scan_points[], scan_progress, scans_without_new, travel_since_seen / last_seen_step per beacon, consecutive_stalls / slips / errors, blocked_heading, mission_start_step, latch, and per-beacon first-observation pose/point (or an obs list) for confirmation. Extend the state enum with `failed`. Specify step 0: PREVIOUS_REPORT = null → pose (0,0,0), empty map. Alternatively, have the harness compute the counters and put them in OBSERVATION.

[2] (major) D3 vs D7 step 3 / step 6 (duplicated IDs)
  QUOTE: A **unique** `id` already in the map → match it. / A **duplicated** or **null** `id` → call `predict_observation` for each candidate beacon.
  PROBLEM: D3 is headed "known to the evaluator, unknown to the agent", and no observation field tells the agent which IDs are duplicated or which condition (unique, duplicated or hidden) the episode runs. When the agent first sees the second physical copy of a duplicated ID, that ID looks "unique already in the map". It is therefore matched with no gate, merged by update_beacon_estimate and possibly used in a pose fix. The only escape is step 6, which applies only to revisits (≥ 1.5 m or ≥ 10 steps) with a discrepancy > 1.0 m and "the ID could be duplicated", a test the agent cannot evaluate either. The spec also never says which D3 rows the prompt's Data ingredient may state: ID range 0–29, the chance of duplicates, arena size, beacon count, and tags on walls at lens height.
  EVIDENCE: AGENT-DESIGN.md L65 (heading), L74 ("Up to 3 IDs ... appear twice"), L197-203, L217-219; D5 OBSERVATION L124-129 has no condition field; PLAN.md L84 says D3 feeds the Data ingredient.
  FIX: Decide one of two options. (a) Gate every ID-matched detection with the same |Δrange| ≤ 0.30 m / |Δbearing| ≤ 15° test, and treat a gate failure on a known ID as a candidate second beacon (`B<id>b`). (b) Add a `condition: unique|duplicated|hidden` field to OBSERVATION. Also add a D3 column 'disclosed to agent: yes/no' for each row.

[3] (major) D7 steps 4-6 ordering
  QUOTE: A matched detection → `update_beacon_estimate`.
  PROBLEM: Step 5 updates the map with every matched detection, whatever step 4 or step 6 decided. Three cases go wrong. (1) A pose fix rejected by the 0.50 m / 20° gate (pose_source prior_kept) still merges the detection, projected from the drifted prior, into the beacon's running mean. (2) A revisit whose discrepancy is between 0.5 and 1.0 m is rejected as a fix but not split off, and still gets merged. (3) Step 6 says "Accept the revisit only through step 4's gates", but step 4 needs ≥ 2 matched confirmed beacons, so a single-beacon revisit can never be accepted, yet it is still merged in step 5. The result is a quiet false loop closure through the map, the exact error RQ2 says L2 cannot absorb. The term "discrepancy" is also undefined: map-point distance, or Δrange/Δbearing?
  EVIDENCE: AGENT-DESIGN.md L204-220; APPROACH-LLM-SLAM.md L100-102 (RQ2: no robust optimiser to absorb a false loop closure).
  FIX: Make step 5 conditional. Merge a detection only if it was used in an accepted pose fix, or if the beacon is tentative and the pose_source is not prior_kept. A detection of a confirmed beacon while pose_source is odometry or prior_kept gets association `rejected` or `ambiguous` and is not merged. Define discrepancy as the distance between the map point and the observation_to_map_point result, and state what happens between 0.5 and 1.0 m.

[4] (major) D10 Budget / D5 'ROSA-compatible'
  QUOTE: so the call fits `vllm-rosa`'s 16,384-token window alongside ROSA's fixed prompts and tool schemas
  PROBLEM: This is stated as fact but was never measured, and the numbers I measured make it doubtful. ROSA loads 47 built-in tool schemas by default (ros2, calculation, system, log), about 18.2k characters of JSON (~4.5-5k tokens), plus about 3.9k characters of fixed prompts. With the D5 tools, a 3k-token robot prompt and a 3k-token step message, that leaves only about 3k tokens for the whole multi-turn tool transcript and Qwen's reasoning output. ROSA's fixed prompt also forces "call tools ONE AT A TIME", so one step is many turns. ROSA's fixed prompts further require "FIRST: Call rosnode_list() and rostopic_list()" for every action request, which conflicts with D7's order and D8's "exactly [one JSON object]". ROSA also defaults to accumulate_chat_history=True, which is not the stateless D5 model.
  EVIDENCE: Measured 2026-09-27 in rosa-ros2 on spark-2a60: `tools 47 schema chars 18244 fixed prompt chars 3881` (convert_to_openai_tool over rosa.tools.{ros2,calculation,system,log}). rosa/prompts.py system_prompts: "WORKFLOW FOR ACTION REQUESTS ... 1. FIRST: Call rosnode_list() and rostopic_list()", "CRITICAL - SEQUENTIAL TOOL EXECUTION". rosa.py L91 `accumulate_chat_history: bool = True`.
  FIX: Record the ROSA demo configuration: blacklist or omit the built-in tool packages, and set accumulate_chat_history=False. Budget the in-step transcript and max output tokens explicitly, e.g. N tool turns × ~150 tokens plus a thinking cap. Either raise --max-model-len (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B supports far more than 16k and the Spark has memory headroom) or measure the token count before claiming the call fits. Note the fixed-prompt conflict as a known ROSA-demo limitation.

[5] (major) D7 thresholds vs D5 tool set (arithmetic)
  QUOTE: Accept the fix if `residual_m` ≤ 0.15 and it lies within 0.50 m and 20° of the prior (DV).
  PROBLEM: Several D7 rules need arithmetic that no D5 tool performs. |Δrange| and |Δbearing| in the association gate need subtraction. The fix-vs-prior distance and the angle-difference wrap need calculation. Cumulative travel of 1.5 m needs summing. "≥ 0.5 m from every earlier scan point" needs distances, and "bearing sector with the fewest mapped beacons within 2 m" needs sectoring. Under ROSA the agent gets ROSA's 30 calculation tools, and under the custom benchmark harness it gets none. So D10's claim that "the same tools" run in both is false, and a single prompt describes two different tool sets.
  EVIDENCE: AGENT-DESIGN.md L143-151 (the seven tools), L199-225; APPROACH-LLM-SLAM.md L91; rosa/prompts.py "You must never perform calculations manually"; rosa/tools/calculation.py has 30 functions (measured in rosa-ros2).
  FIX: Either have predict_observation and pose_from_beacons return the gate quantities directly (Δrange, Δbearing, and distance and Δθ to a supplied prior), or add one bounded `check_gates` / `distance(a,b)` tool. Also have the harness supply travel_since and scan-point distances in the observation. State that ROSA's built-in calculation tools are excluded so that both runtimes expose the identical D5 tool set.

[9] (minor) D7 step 6 vs step 7 (scan-induced revisits)
  QUOTE: a *revisit* is a confirmed beacon seen again after ≥ 1.5 m of travel, or ≥ 10 steps out of view.
  PROBLEM: A scan is 12 consecutive 30° turns in place. A beacon that is in the camera's field of view for about 2–3 headings is out of view for about 9–10 steps and then reappears at the end of the same scan, with zero travel. The "≥ 10 steps out of view" clause can therefore label an in-place rotation a loop closure. That inflates the loop_closure events that T2 scores (revisit precision and recall).
  EVIDENCE: AGENT-DESIGN.md L215-216, L222-223; APPROACH-LLM-SLAM.md L199 (T2 revisit precision/recall).
  FIX: Define a revisit by travel only (≥ 1.5 m of travel since last seen), or exclude re-sightings made within the same scan or at the same scan point.

  PROBLEM: Duplicates are unknown to the agent (D3), so the first sighting of the second physical copy of a duplicated ID is by definition a "unique id already in the map". Step 3 matches it with no gate. Step 5 (update_beacon_estimate) then averages the wrong point into B<id>, before step 6 gets a chance to spawn B<id>b. Also: (a) step 6 only runs for a "revisit", so a first sighting from a new place never triggers it. (b) "discrepancy" is undefined: fix-vs-prior, or new point vs map point? (c) A discrepancy of 0.5–1.0 m fails the step-4 gate but is below the 1.0 m second-beacon threshold, and no rule covers it. (d) "the ID could be duplicated" is always true under the duplicated-ID condition and never knowable. (e) D3 sets no minimum separation between the two copies of an ID, so two copies placed within the 0.30 m / 15° gate cannot be told apart.
  EVIDENCE: Research condition 4 (the duplicated-ID condition) is a headline condition in D3. Example: B3 was mapped at (1.8, 0.3); the robot later sees the other ID 3 at a spot 2 m away. Step 3 → matched. Step 5 → B3 running mean moves about 1 m toward the midpoint (n_obs=1 → 2). Both copies are now wrongly mapped.
  FIX: Gate every match, unique IDs included. Compute predict_observation for the map entry. Within 0.30 m / 15° → matched. Discrepancy (map point vs observation_to_map_point of the detection) above an explicit threshold D (for example 1.0 m) → create B<id>b as tentative. Between gate and D → `rejected`, no map update. Do this in step 3, before steps 4 and 5. Define discrepancy as that point-to-point distance. In D3, require the two copies of a duplicated ID to be ≥ 1.5 m apart.

[19] (major) D5 pose_from_beacons + D7 step 4
  QUOTE: `pose_from_beacons(matches)` | ≥ 2 pairs of (map point, range, bearing) **from this step only** → `{x_m, y_m, theta_deg, residual_m}` ... Accept the fix if `residual_m` ≤ 0.15
  PROBLEM: Two range+bearing landmarks do fix a 2D pose (4 equations for 3 unknowns, and the bearings remove the mirror solution). But with only 1 redundant degree of freedom, the geometry is badly conditioned whenever the two beacons are close in angle, and a single camera with about 60° FOV always sees them close in angle. The cross-range position and the heading trade off against each other, and only the range difference constrains that trade-off. A small residual_m does not show this, because the residual has one degree of freedom. residual_m itself is also undefined: range and bearing residuals have different units and no weighting is given. The fix replaces the prior outright, so a poorly conditioned fix overwrites a better odometry prior.
  EVIDENCE: Linearised covariance, σ_bearing = 2°, beacons 2 m ahead. Separation 1.0 m (28° apart), σ_r = 0.10 → σ_y = 0.29 m, σ_θ = 8.0°. Separation 0.6 m (17°) → σ_y = 0.48 m, σ_θ = 13°. At 2.5 m with σ_r = 0.20 (topic 2: 30–45 cm at 3 m), separation 1.0 m → σ_y = 0.72 m, σ_θ = 16°. Typical beacon spacing implied by D3 (6–12 tags on an 8–12 m perimeter) is 0.7–2 m.
  FIX: Define residual_m as the RMS distance between each map point and observation_to_map_point(fix, range, bearing). Weight by range, for example σ_r ∝ range. Have the tool also return the fix's std (σ_xy, σ_θ) or GDOP. Add acceptance conditions: angular separation ≥ 30° or ≥ 3 beacons, every range ≤ 2.0 m, and σ below thresholds. Otherwise report `prior_kept`.

[20] (major) D7 step 4 and step 6 (resection acceptance and loop closure)
  QUOTE: Accept the fix if `residual_m` ≤ 0.15 and it lies within 0.50 m and 20° of the prior (DV). Otherwise keep the prior ... Accept the revisit only through step 4's gates.
  PROBLEM: There is no recovery path. Once the dead-reckoned prior is more than 0.5 m or 20° from the truth, every correct fix fails the gate, so the pose becomes `prior_kept` or `odometry` permanently and keeps drifting. The cause can be a missed stall, wheel spin against a wall, a slip, or a wrong fix accepted earlier. The gated duplicate-ID association uses the same drifting prior, so the error compounds. Loop closure, whose purpose is to correct accumulated drift, can by construction correct at most 0.5 m / 20°. It also corrects only the current pose: beacons mapped along the drifted segment keep their error. That is relocalisation, not loop closure, and the spec should say so.
  EVIDENCE: One undetected 0.25 m forward move pushing a wall, plus modest drift, puts the prior 0.5 m off. After that, two consistent fixes from well-separated confirmed unique beacons, each with residual 0.03 m, are both rejected indefinitely.
  FIX: Add a relocalisation rule. Accept a fix that fails the prior gate if either (a) it has ≥ 3 confirmed unique-ID beacons with a low residual, or (b) two consecutive steps give fixes that agree with each other, after compose_odometry between them, within 0.15 m / 5°. Report it as `pose_source: relocalised` and `loop_closure.accepted=true`. State in the prompt that closures correct only the current pose.

[21] (major) D7 step 5 (Update the map)
  QUOTE: A matched detection → `update_beacon_estimate`.
  PROBLEM: The map update runs whatever pose_source is. When step 4 set `prior_kept`, the agent has just found the prior inconsistent with these very beacons. Averaging new points computed from that prior into the confirmed beacons feeds the inconsistency into the map. With `odometry` poses, confirmed beacons absorb dead-reckoning drift, and the equal-weight running mean makes the map follow the drift. When the pose came from a fix built from the same detections, the update adds almost no information and double-counts. The mean is also unweighted: a 2.5 m sighting (±0.3–0.45 m) counts as much as a 0.7 m sighting (±0.04 m).
  EVIDENCE: D7 step 4 has three outcomes (beacon_fix / odometry / prior_kept). Step 5 applies to all three with no condition. Range-error scaling is from topic 2 (Hinderer 2025; Abbas 2019).
  FIX: Do not update beacons that fed the fix. Do not update confirmed beacons when pose_source is prior_kept. When pose_source is odometry, update only tentative beacons. Add a weight to update_beacon_estimate (for example w = quality / range²), or skip detections with range > 2 m or quality < 0.5.

[22] (major) D6 Wall rule
  QUOTE: **Wall rule:** no `FORWARD_25` when any detection with |bearing| ≤ 15° has range < 0.60 m; no `FORWARD_10` when it has range < 0.35 m (DV; tags are on walls).
  PROBLEM: Range to a tag is not the distance to the wall along the heading. Two consequences. (1) Gaps: at 0.60 m from a wall, a tag is inside ±15° only if the robot is within 0.6·tan15° = 0.16 m laterally of it. Between tags the rule never fires. (2) Oblique walls: with the wall normal at angle α to the heading and a tag at bearing β, the distance ahead to the wall is t = r·cos(β−α)/cos α. For r = 0.60, β = +15°, α = −60°, t = 0.31 m. For α = −70°, t = 0.15 m. FORWARD_25 is allowed in both cases and drives into the wall. The D6 table calls the residual risk "an untagged wall hit", but under this rule most of every wall counts as untagged.
  EVIDENCE: With 1 m tag spacing, the covered fraction of a wall for a head-on approach at 0.6 m is about 2·0.16/1.0 ≈ 32%. The oblique-wall numbers follow from the formula above. D3 already mounts tags flat on the walls, and AprilTag detection gives the tag's full pose, but D3 says "Tag orientation is not used in v1".
  FIX: Have the detector pipeline output each tag's normal, or a derived `wall_ahead_m` = distance along the robot's heading to the plane of any detected tag, using all detections, not only those within |bearing| ≤ 15°. Apply the 0.60 / 0.35 m thresholds to that value. Failing that, also use mapped confirmed beacons to predict wall lines, since beacons on the same wall are collinear. The prompt must state that the rule is blind between tags.

[23] (major) D2 GO_TO_BEACON done condition + D7 step 7 + D6
  QUOTE: Predicted range to it ≤ 0.45 m (DV standoff) and |bearing| ≤ 15° ... then `FORWARD_25` (or `FORWARD_10` inside 0.60 m) until the D2 done condition holds.
  PROBLEM: Done is judged on the map-predicted range, but the wall rule is judged on the detected range. Near the target, with a narrow FOV at about 0.5 m, usually only the target tag is visible, so pose_from_beacons (≥ 2 beacons) cannot run and the prediction is never corrected. If the map point is off by about 0.2 m along the approach (normal decimetre error), the mission deadlocks: predicted 0.50 m says keep going, detected 0.30 m means FORWARD_10 is forbidden, and turning breaks the bearing condition. The agent loops until the 60-step budget runs out. In the opposite case (predicted too short), it stops at a true 0.65 m and reports success.
  EVIDENCE: Nominal kinematic simulation with a perfect map: every start converges (worst case 33 steps). The failure comes only from map/prediction error, which D7 step 4 cannot fix with a single visible beacon.
  FIX: When the target tag is detected, use the detected range and bearing for both the done test and the approach. Use predict_observation only while the target is out of view. Treat "detected range < 0.45 m" as done, so the wall rule and done cannot conflict.

[24] (major) D7 step 7 (go-to heading window) + D2 done
  QUOTE: turn until the target's predicted |bearing| ≤ 15°, then `FORWARD_25`
  PROBLEM: The only turns are ±30°, and the ±15° acceptance window is exactly half a turn, so there is zero margin. A consistent turn overshoot (executed 31–33°) makes the robot swap between bearing +15+ε and −15−ε forever. The GO_TO done condition (|bearing| ≤ 15°) has the same problem. The spec also never says which way to turn (positive bearing → TURN_LEFT_30). A sign slip costs a full circle.
  EVIDENCE: Simulated GO_TO from random starts 0.6–2.5 m away, turns with a systematic bias and no noise: +1° → 26% fail (budget exhausted), +2° → 47%, +3° → 60%. With a ±20° window, 0% fail for the same biases. Random unbiased noise breaks the cycle (0% fail), but the executor is expected to have a systematic bias until calibrated in W3.
  FIX: Use a window of |bearing| ≤ 20° to start driving, and ≤ 20° for GO_TO done, which is larger than half the turn size plus the expected bias. Alternatively add TURN_LEFT_10 / TURN_RIGHT_10. State the rule "bearing > 0 → TURN_LEFT_30, bearing < 0 → TURN_RIGHT_30".

[25] (major) D7 step 6 (revisit definition)
  QUOTE: a *revisit* is a confirmed beacon seen again after ≥ 1.5 m of travel, or ≥ 10 steps out of view.
  PROBLEM: Every 360° scan (12 × 30°) turns each beacon out of view for about 10 steps, because a ~60° FOV sees a given bearing for only about 2 of the 12 headings. So the "≥ 10 steps out of view" branch fires for every confirmed beacon on every scan with zero travel. Each such sighting then counts as a loop-closure candidate. That inflates the closure counts the RQ2 precision metric relies on, and applies the >1.0 m second-beacon logic to trivial re-sightings.
  EVIDENCE: FOV about 60–65° (topic 2 assumption). One beacon's visible headings in a 12-step scan: 2 → out of view for 10 steps ≥ threshold.
  FIX: Define a revisit by travel only (for example odometer ≥ 1.5 m since last seen), or by steps out of view counting only translational moves. Exclude re-sightings within the same scan.

[26] (major) D7 step 7 (exploration heuristic) and step 8 (termination)
  QUOTE: Otherwise head toward the least-observed direction, i.e. the bearing sector with the fewest mapped beacons within 2 m. ... Terminate EXPLORE with `DONE` when: two consecutive scans ... ; every beacon is `confirmed`, or has been re-observed and failed
  PROBLEM: (1) Sector width and tie-breaking are undefined, and the rule depends only on the map, so from two places it can send the robot back and forth between the same two scan points indefinitely. (2) All beacons are on walls, so "fewest mapped beacons" deliberately points the robot at the wall section with no tags, the one place the tag-based wall rule is blind. (3) Whether the three termination bullets are AND or OR is ambiguous; presumably (A and B) or C. (4) The DONE condition can be unreachable. A tentative beacon seen once (a false detection, or a far sighting) that is never re-observed blocks "every beacon confirmed" forever, so only the budget ends the episode, which distorts steps-to-done. (5) Step 7 (choose and execute a move) comes before step 8 (DONE), but D8 allows only one decision per step. Termination is guaranteed only by the 150-step budget.
  EVIDENCE: Budget arithmetic: 12-step scan + ≥ 2 FORWARD_25 = 14 steps per new place, so at most about 10 scans in 150 steps. DONE is feasible in about 40 steps only if every tentative beacon is re-observed.
  FIX: Define sectors as 12 × 30° in the map frame, ties broken by the farthest unvisited scan-grid cell. Score frontier cells (a coarse 0.5 m grid of unvisited scan points) instead of beacon counts, and exclude directions whose predicted wall is less than 0.6 m away. Write the stop rule as (A AND B) OR budget. Mark a tentative beacon `unconfirmable` if a later scan had its predicted position within FOV and within 2 m and it was not detected. Evaluate termination before choosing a move.

[27] (major) D7 gates vs L2 principle (tools do arithmetic)
  QUOTE: Accept the fix if `residual_m` ≤ 0.15 and it lies within 0.50 m and 20° of the prior ... (≥ 0.5 m from every earlier scan point) ... agree within 0.30 m

[32] (major) D2 / D9 — STOP mission and the halt latch
  QUOTE: | `STOP` | Stop now | Immediately; a latch that only the operator clears |
  PROBLEM: The operator's STOP is defined as a mission the LLM classifies from plain language (D2 heading: "Missions, as the operator may phrase them in plain language"; D9: "may say STOP at any time"). With stateless per-step calls (D5/D10) and a blocking `move` tool, operator text only reaches the model at the start of the next step. So STOP cannot act during a move or during the model's thinking time, and it depends on the model classifying it correctly. `emergency_stop` is also a tool only the LLM calls. The spec never says where the latch lives (Pico RAM, validator, or harness process) or how the operator clears it. A latch held in Pico RAM is cleared by any Pico reset. A latch held in the harness is cleared by a harness restart.
  EVIDENCE: research topic3 line 39/51: decision latency is about 1–2 s at best and "minutes at max effort". D5: `move(action)` returns `{result: done|stalled|timeout|refused, executed}`, i.e. the call blocks until the move ends. car-build-notes.md 'Watch items': the bent battery-tray spring is the "First suspect on any brownout or mid-run motor cut", so a Pico reset mid-run is a known risk.
  FIX: Make operator STOP an out-of-band path that bypasses the LLM, e.g. a harness hotkey or a ROS topic `/estop`. It should (a) send an abort to the Pico that cancels any move in progress and drops STBY low, and (b) set a persistent latch in the validator, written to disk. Clearing it takes an explicit operator command outside the LLM. The Pico should also boot latched (STBY low, moves refused until an arm command). Keep the STOP mission in the prompt only as a secondary path the model must obey and report. The battery switch stays the final layer.

[33] (major) D6 layer 3 — wall rule and error rules enforced only by the prompt
  QUOTE: | `move(action)` | one D4 action → `{result: done\|stalled\|timeout\|refused, executed}` | The validator refuses actions outside D4, or any action while the halt latch is set |
  PROBLEM: The only guard against driving into a tagged wall is the wall rule ("no `FORWARD_25` when any detection with |bearing| ≤ 15° has range < 0.60 m…"), and it is an LLM guardrail only. The validator does not check it. The same holds for "No move when the observation has `status: error`", "Two stalls in a row … → `emergency_stop`" and "Never exceed the step budget". The benchmark exists because LLMs are expected to fail at spatial and metric reasoning, so the design leans on the least reliable layer for collision avoidance. Each of these checks is a deterministic, single-observation test the validator can run at no cost to the L2 research question.
  EVIDENCE: research topic5 lines 18–20: LLMs collapse on long-horizon state tracking ("Every tested LLM scored 0% on the long-horizon tasks"). D6 itself calls the Pico and validator layers the ones "below the LLM", yet it lists the wall rule only under "Agent rules (the prompt's Guardrails)".
  FIX: Add all of these to the `move` validator, using the current step's OBSERVATION: the detection-based wall rule, no move on `status: error`, auto-latch after 2 consecutive `stalled` or `slip_suspected`, and a hard step-budget cutoff. Return `refused` with a reason. Keep the same rules in the prompt, and log each validator refusal as a guardrail violation. That turns the safety net into a metric (guardrail compliance) instead of a hazard.

[34] (major) D6 layer 2 — stall detection as the only contact signal
  QUOTE: the encoder count falls below 50 % (DV) of expected during a move → stop,
  PROBLEM: (1) Encoder-based stall detection catches only locked wheels. If the car hits a wall and the wheels slip on a smooth floor, or the light car pushes a cardboard wall along, the encoders count normally. The move ends `done` with odometry that reports motion that never happened. The IMU check (`slip_suspected` = encoder yaw vs IMU yaw > 10°) catches only a rotational disagreement, and a square-on hit gives ~0 yaw on both. So the D6 table's claim "Residual risk: an untagged wall hit at low speed, stopped by stall detection" is not true for the slip case. (2) Neither the detection window nor a start-up grace period is defined. Every move starts from rest with counts below the expected profile, so a naive check gives false stalls, and a loose one leaves both motors stalled for up to the 5 s timeout. (3) "Expected" is undefined: if it comes from commanded PWM, sagging alkaline cells will trip it.
  EVIDENCE: motor-encoder-cable-map.md §6: stall current 0.82 A per motor; "Do not hold the wheels stalled." tb6612fng-pin-map.md: "No over-current / short-circuit protection"; "both motors stalled can overheat the chip". The BNO055 is the only other motion sensor (no range sensor, no current sense on the TB6612FNG board).
  FIX: Specify the stall detector: a closed-loop speed profile on encoders, a start-up grace of about 150–300 ms (DV), then "measured < 50 % of the profile over a 200 ms window (DV)" → stop within ≤ 0.3 s (DV). Add a second contact/no-progress signal that does not rely on the wheels: a BNO055 linear-acceleration spike (impact) during a move → `result: bumped`, and/or a single-step visual check in the observation builder (a tag tracked before and after a FORWARD move should close by about the encoder distance; if not → `quality: no_progress`). Rewrite the residual-risk sentence to say a wheel-slip contact is not detected by stall detection. Verify the detector against a fixed wall at low speed in W3.

[37] (major) D7 step 7 / D6 wall rule — approaching a wall-mounted beacon on the belief pose
  QUOTE: `FORWARD_25` (or `FORWARD_10` inside 0.60 m) until the D2 done condition holds.
