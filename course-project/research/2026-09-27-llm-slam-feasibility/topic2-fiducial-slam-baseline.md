SUMMARY: Classical fiducial SLAM is a mature, well-supported baseline for this robot. TagSLAM (Pfrommer & Daniilidis 2019, arXiv:1910.00679) is a GTSAM front end that uses AprilTags and optional odometry. It has a ROS 2 branch, still marked "work in progress", that needs Jazzy or newer. The same design is easy to hand-build with GTSAM's planar SLAM factors, or as an EKF-SLAM with known correspondence. Unique tag IDs largely remove the data-association problem: TagSLAM says loop closure is "guaranteed once a tag is successfully decoded". The risks that remain are wrong loop closures from human error (duplicate IDs, wrong tag size) and pose ambiguity. On accuracy, no source gives numbers for an ESP32-CAM/OV3660, but close analogues show how things scale. A 1080p webcam with 15 cm markers gave about 3–5 cm RMSE from a single marker at 1.3 m, about 30–45 cm at 3 m, and about 4 cm when 5 or more markers were visible (Hinderer et al. 2025). A single AprilTag at 70 cm gave about 4.4 cm raw x-error (Abbas et al. 2019). A Duckiebot EKF-SLAM with no wheel encoders had about 0.25 m mean landmark error, and did better when it used only tags closer than 90 cm (SLAMDuck). The camera link is the tightest limit. An OV2640 ESP32-CAM streaming over HTTP reached about 14 fps at 640x480 and about 8 fps at 800x600; OV3660 users report roughly 15 fps at most, with latency around 80 ms or more. The software runs on the Spark's aarch64: apriltag_ros, GTSAM, image_proc and camera_calibration all have arm64 ROS 2 Jazzy binaries, GTSAM 4.3.0 also has aarch64 PyPI wheels, and isaac_ros_apriltag officially supports DGX Spark, but only on ROS 2 Lyrical. Overall, a classical tag-SLAM should plausibly reach map and trajectory errors of several centimetres up to a few decimetres in a small arena. That makes it a meaningful reference for an LLM estimator, but the exact number has to be measured on this hardware rather than assumed.

IMPLICATIONS:
- INFERENCE (feasibility): The fixed hardware supports a classical landmark-SLAM baseline. Compute is not the limit; the limits are the camera (resolution × frame rate × latency over 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi) and the odometry. Aim for a baseline in the 'several cm to a few dm' range in a small arena, not mm-level. No source measures this exact camera, so the baseline error must be measured on the real robot and written into the benchmark as the reference.
- INFERENCE (recommended classical baseline, B1): a hand-written Python GTSAM planar factor graph. Pose2 per keyframe; one Point2 landmark per AprilTag, keyed by tag ID; BetweenFactor from encoder odometry, with yaw from the BNO055 in IMUPLUS mode (no magnetometer); BearingRangeFactor from each apriltag_ros detection, using only the tag-centre translation turned into range and bearing (with the camera extrinsic). Solve with iSAM2 online and a full batch at the end. This is short, fully inspectable, uses the same inputs the LLM sees, and installs from the aarch64 PyPI wheel or ros-jazzy-gtsam.
- INFERENCE (secondary baselines): (B0) dead-reckoning only, the lower bound. (B2) EKF-SLAM with known correspondence, the didactic textbook version. (B3) TagSLAM ROS 2, an independent off-the-shelf reference; its ROS 2 port is marked WIP, so budget time or treat it as optional. A good LLM should fall between B0 and B1. Beating B0 is the minimum success criterion; approaching B1 would be strong.
- INFERENCE (camera pipeline): ESP32-CAM JPEG at VGA (about 10–15 fps expected, from the OV2640 data plus OV3660 anecdotes) → a small HTTP/MJPEG-to-ROS bridge publishing CompressedImage with timestamps → image_proc rectification, using intrinsics from ros-jazzy-camera-calibration → apriltag_ros with the CPU backend. isaac_ros_apriltag is unnecessary at these frame rates, and it only officially supports ROS 2 Lyrical on DGX OS 7.2.3; check which ROS 2 distro the Spark actually runs.
- INFERENCE (stop-and-look protocol): with roughly 80–150 ms latency, ~10 fps, motion blur and TT-motor backlash, the robot should stop after each bounded move and collect N frames (e.g. 5) while stationary. It then averages the detections per tag and hands one observation set to both the classical estimator and the LLM. This also fits naturally with LLM turn-taking and makes the two estimators get identical evidence.
- INFERENCE (tag sizing): apriltag-esp32 detects a 5 cm tag at over 1.5 m at 800 px wide, so detection starts at roughly 20 px across the tag. At 640 px wide, with an assumed ~60–65° horizontal FOV (the real FOV of this OV3660 module is unknown), a 15 cm tag is about 38 px at 2 m and about 25 px at 3 m. That is detectable, but pose/range quality will be poor, given Hinderer's 30–45 cm single-marker RMSE at 3 m with 1080p. Recommendation: tags of at least 15–20 cm (measured at the black/white border), mostly used within about 2 m, and density such that 2–3 or more tags are usually in view.
- INFERENCE (ambiguity mitigation): for a ground robot, constrain the estimate to SE(2) (fixed camera height, roll and pitch). Use tag translation (range, bearing) rather than inverting the full tag rotation, because a rotation flip gets amplified by the lever arm into large position error. Gate out detections with low decision_margin, hamming>0, very small pixel area, or near-frontal small tags, following TagSLAM's ambiguity gate. Mount tags vertically at camera height on varied walls or stands.
- INFERENCE (data association and benchmark design): unique 36h11 IDs make data association and loop closure trivial for both classical and LLM estimators. With IDs given, the benchmark mainly tests metric fusion (odometry plus noisy range-bearing), which is exactly where LLMs are expected to be weakest. To test 'recognising revisits', add conditions such as: (a) IDs hidden, where the model gets 'a tag' plus geometry only, or a VLM must read the raw image; (b) a few deliberately duplicated IDs; (c) raw JPEG frames with no detector at all, for VLM/VLA tracks.
- INFERENCE (odometry): 4320 counts/rev (x4) gives far more resolution than needed. Systematic errors (unequal wheel diameter, effective wheelbase) will dominate, so run a UMBmark-style square-path calibration before collecting any benchmark data. Use BNO055 IMUPLUS (gyro-only) yaw to avoid the motor-magnet problem on the compass.
- INFERENCE (ground truth): in simulation (Gazebo Harmonic / Isaac Sim), use exact simulator poses and tag positions. In the real world, measure tag positions by hand (tape/laser) as the map ground truth, following TagSLAM's use of laser-measured tags. For trajectory ground truth, use a Duckietown-style overhead 'watchtower' webcam that tracks one or more AprilTags on the robot's roof, mounted low (about 1.3–2 m) or with several roof tags. Hinderer shows a single marker at 3 m is not good enough for ground truth.
- INFERENCE (metrics): report ATE and RPE (via evo from ROS 2 bags), landmark-map RMSE after SE(2)/Umeyama alignment keyed by tag ID, loop-closure correctness, number of lost/diverged runs, per-step decision latency, and token/$ cost. Report each LLM relative to B0 and B1 on identical logged observation sequences. Replaying the same logged bags to every model makes the comparison fair and repeatable, and offline replay makes the many-model comparison cheap.
- INFERENCE (risks): the ESP32-CAM and Pico 2 W micro-ROS share the 2.4 GHz band, so expect latency spikes. OV3660 driver frame-rate issues are reported. JPEG above CIF needs PSRAM. A wrong printed tag size scales every range. Lighting and motion blur cause missed detections. The TagSLAM ROS 2 port is WIP. None of these threaten feasibility; all argue for stop-and-look operation and offline bag replay.
- INFERENCE (optional variant): run AprilTag detection on the ESP32 itself (apriltag-esp32) and send only IDs and corners. This cuts bandwidth and latency, but the rate is limited to about 5–10 Hz, the family is limited to 35 36h11 IDs, and it uses the camera MCU's memory. It is worth trying only if Wi-Fi JPEG streaming turns out to be the bottleneck.

CHALLENGES:
- 'Several cm to a few dm' baseline: the sources don't carry over to this robot. Hinderer uses 1080p ArUco looking straight down from the ceiling; Abbas's 4.4 cm is an 'ideal scenario' with a raw variance of 112 cm²; SLAMDuck's figures are signed biases in a student README. A rough pixel-scaling INFERENCE: 640 px is about 3x fewer pixels per axis than 1080p, so Hinderer's single-marker 1.33 m case (3–5 cm) maps to roughly 0.45 m at VGA, and the 3 m case (30–45 cm) to roughly 1 m, if the fields of view are similar. The low end of the range is optimistic for single-tag views beyond about 1 m.
- Tag sizing (about 20 px detection threshold; use tags within about 2 m): the 20 px figure comes from apriltag-esp32's '>1.5M ... might even be further, but haven't tested'. That is a lower bound on range, not a detection threshold. The FOV used for the conversion is also assumed. Scaling Hinderer as above, 2 m single-tag ranging at VGA would likely be decimetre-level, not a comfortable working range. Recommend measuring range error against distance on the real camera before fixing the tag layout.
- Ambiguity mitigation 'following TagSLAM's ambiguity gate': apriltag_ros publishes only the homography, hamming and decision_margin, not the two-solution pose-ambiguity error that TagSLAM gates on. Copying the gate needs a custom detector wrapper (for example apriltag's estimate_tag_pose, which returns err, or IPPE). The sources also disagree on where ambiguity is worst: TagSLAM and Duckietown say 'shallow angle', Hinderer and Ch'ng say frontal. The gating rule should be tested, not assumed.
- 'A good LLM should fall between B0 and B1': no source supports this expectation. An LLM doing metric fusion could plausibly do worse than dead-reckoning (B0). Present it as a hypothesis for the benchmark to test, not a prior.
- Offline bag replay as the main many-model comparison: replay tests only the estimation part. The project also wants the LLM to 'decide where to explore next'. That is closed-loop, so every model's choices change what it observes, and a fixed bag can't score it. Exploration needs closed-loop runs in simulation, with replay kept for estimation-only tracks.
- Overhead 'watchtower' webcam as real-world trajectory ground truth: the ground truth must be several times more accurate than the thing it measures. Hinderer's single 1080p ceiling camera at 1.3 m already gives 3–5 cm RMSE, and 45 cm at 3 m with one marker. Autolab reports only repeatability (1.3–3.4 cm SD), not accuracy. So a single webcam ground truth could be about as accurate as B1 and unable to rank estimators near B1. Plan several roof tags, a multi-camera setup, or at least hand-measured waypoint checks, and state the ground-truth error in the benchmark.
- The isaac_ros_apriltag point is right in substance, with one addition: Lyrical's Tier 1 Linux platform is Ubuntu 26.04. A Jazzy/Noble Spark would need containers or an OS change, which further supports using apriltag_ros on Jazzy.
- esp32-cam-fpv as evidence that HTTP is the bottleneck, and as an optional path: it depends on monitor-mode packet injection to a dedicated receiver. That is incompatible with the normal Wi-Fi network the Pico micro-ROS link uses, so it is not a drop-in fix. The 50–80 ms latency cannot be used as an estimate for HTTP streaming.
- The 80–150 ms latency assumption rests on an ESP32-S3 micro-ROS snippet (78 ms median, resolution unknown) and non-HTTP FPV figures. No source measured HTTP/MJPEG latency for an ESP32 with an OV3660. The stop-and-look protocol is still sensible, but the numbers are a guess.
- Data association 'trivial for both classical and LLM estimators': true only when the LLM is given decoded IDs. The main SLAMDuck failure was systematic range bias, not association. So the benchmark's hard part is metric fusion, which the researcher does acknowledge. The duplicated-ID and hidden-ID conditions are well motivated.

UNKNOWNS:
- No published measurement of OV3660 (as opposed to OV2640) ESP32-CAM JPEG frame rate and end-to-end latency at VGA/SVGA over HTTP. The only OV3660 evidence is anecdotal GitHub issues (~15 fps max).
- No published AprilTag pose/range accuracy study using an ESP32-CAM or OV3660 image stream. The accuracy numbers above come from 1080p webcams (Hinderer), unspecified cameras (Abbas), or synthetic/industrial cameras (Olson, Wang).
