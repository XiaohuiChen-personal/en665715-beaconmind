SUMMARY: Yes, a credible benchmark is possible, and every metric it needs already exists. For localisation and mapping there are ATE (align the trajectories with Horn/Umeyama, then take the RMSE) and RPE (drift over a fixed interval) from Sturm et al. 2012, both implemented in evo, which reads ROS 2 bags directly. Kümmerle et al. 2009 note that metrics built on landmark positions need known data association (which estimated landmark is which real one). AprilTag IDs supply that for free, so tag-position RMSE is a valid map metric here. For exploration and navigation, Anderson et al. 2018 (arXiv:1807.06757) define success and SPL: a DONE action, a success radius of 2× body width, geodesic path lengths, and an explicit statement of which sensors the agent gets. Coverage (Chaplot 2020) and the times to reach 90% and 99% exploration (Xu 2022, Explore-Bench) measure exploration efficiency. Existing LLM/VLM embodied benchmarks, for example EmbodiedBench (temperature 0, one run, 20-step cap), mostly report one-shot success rates with no statistical testing. Spatial-reasoning benchmarks consistently find that LLMs break down on long-horizon sequential spatial tracking (PPNL, Lost in Aggregation, CubeBench). Structured memories or explicit cognitive maps help (Thinking on Maps, VSI-Bench). Simulation tooling is available: a Gazebo Harmonic AprilTag model generator; OdometryPublisher for ground-truth pose (DiffDrive odometry for realistic, error-prone odometry); ros_gz_bridge and ros_gz_sim create; and in Isaac Sim 5.1, an AprilTag MDL material, a ground-truth transform publisher and Replicator randomisation (5.1 is flagged as unsupported). For fair comparisons with few episodes, the sources agree on paired designs (Miller 2024) and bootstrap confidence intervals (with the caveat that they are too narrow at about 3 runs). They also recommend exact McNemar for binary outcomes, Wilcoxon for two systems and Friedman for many (Demšar 2006), and Holm correction. Real-robot trials should be interleaved and blind (Kress-Gazit 2024), sequential stopping rules (STEP) can cut the number of trials, and sim–real agreement can be checked with MMRV plus Pearson (SIMPLER). LLM outputs vary between runs even at temperature 0 (Atil 2024; Thinking Machines), so each world seed needs repeated runs.

IMPLICATIONS:
- INFERENCE (metric suite): score each episode on four layers. (A) Localisation and map: ATE-RMSE after SE(2) Umeyama alignment of the pose belief the LLM commits at each decision step, computed with evo from a ROS 2 bag; RPE per 1 m; tag-map RMSE after the same alignment (valid because tag IDs give data association for free); tag-ID precision/recall to catch hallucinated or missed tags; loop-closure precision/recall against ground-truth revisits. (B) Exploration: share of tags found and share of area covered as functions of distance, steps and wall-clock time, plus time to 90% and 99% of tags found. (C) Post-mapping navigation ('go to tag k', 'return home') using the agent's own map: success and SPL with a DONE action, a 2× body-width threshold and geodesics from the ground-truth occupancy grid. (D) Cost: tokens, $ per episode, latency per decision, and the rate of invalid tool calls.
- INFERENCE (the key to credibility): run two evaluation modes. (1) Open-loop replay: record sensor logs once, in sim and on the real robot (odometry, AprilTag detections as ID+range+bearing, JPEG frames), and feed the identical sequence to every model. Every model then sees the same observations, so ATE and map error differ only by reasoning, and replay is cheap to repeat. (2) Closed-loop: the model also chooses where to go, which tests exploration. Without mode 1, a model that explored badly also sees fewer tags, and its ATE is confounded.
- INFERENCE (baselines): include wheel-odometry dead-reckoning as the floor; a classical EKF-SLAM or GTSAM/TagSLAM estimator on the same tag detections as the reference ceiling; random and frontier exploration; and an oracle-pose condition that isolates exploration and decision quality from localisation. Without a classical baseline, 'LLM as SLAM' results cannot be interpreted.
- INFERENCE (isolate reasoning from perception): give all text-LLM conditions the same AprilTag detector outputs. Add a VLM image-only condition, where the model must detect and read tags itself, as a separate ablation. State inputs explicitly (odometry given or not, IMU heading given or not), as Anderson et al. require. Given the Thinking on Maps and VSI-Bench results, also compare 'structured map-memory tool' against 'raw history in context', because the memory scaffold may matter more than the model.
- INFERENCE (VLA/navigation foundation models): end-to-end VLA or navigation-diffusion policies (e.g. NoMaD) do not output a pose or a landmark map. Score them only on layers B and C as exploration baselines, not in the ATE or map tables.
- INFERENCE (sim tooling for this project): use Gazebo Harmonic with ROS 2 Jazzy (the officially paired distro) as the main generator of many seeded worlds. A small Python generator can write seeded SDF arenas (walls and boxes, N tag36h11 models from the Harmonic gazebo_apriltag fork at random wall poses) or spawn them with ros_gz_sim create. OdometryPublisher (noise 0) provides ground truth and DiffDrive provides realistic, error-prone odometry, all bridged with ros_gz_bridge. SDF2MAP produces the occupancy grid used for coverage and geodesics. Separate dev seeds (for prompt tuning) from held-out test seeds (Kapoor et al.). Use Isaac Sim 5.1 (AprilTag.mdl, Raw Transform Tree for ground truth, Replicator randomisation) only for a small photoreal subset: 5.1 is flagged unsupported, live-streaming does not work on aarch64, and the Gazebo pipeline alone already yields a valid benchmark.
- INFERENCE (ESP32-CAM realism): in sim, degrade camera frames to match the OV3660 over Wi-Fi (low resolution, JPEG artefacts, latency, dropped frames). Measure the real detector's tag range, bearing noise and detection range once, and inject the same noise into the simulated detections, so the sim-to-real step does not change the estimator's inputs.
- INFERENCE (episode counts; my arithmetic with scipy exact tests): with n paired episodes, the smallest two-sided p-value an exact Wilcoxon or sign/McNemar test can reach is 2/2^n. So n=5 gives at best 0.0625, n=6 gives 0.031, n=9 gives 0.0039 and n=10 gives 0.0020. Comparing 6 models pairwise means 15 tests, so Holm's first threshold is 0.05/15 ≈ 0.0033, which needs at least 10 paired episodes even if one model wins every episode. Real-world trials should therefore test only 2–3 pre-registered comparisons (e.g. best frontier model vs best local model vs classical baseline) with at least 10 paired episodes each, or use STEP/SAVI sequential stopping. The full many-model comparison belongs in sim, e.g. about 30–50 held-out seeds × K≥3 repeats.
- INFERENCE (analysis plan to pre-register): name one primary endpoint per mode (e.g. ATE-RMSE in replay, tags found within a fixed budget in closed loop). Run Friedman across all models, then pairwise Wilcoxon on per-seed means with Holm correction. Report paired, world-clustered bootstrap percentile CIs of the differences. Test binary success with exact McNemar, or Cochran's Q for many models. Report IQM next to the mean because failures cause heavy tails, and show cost-versus-accuracy Pareto plots. Use a pilot to estimate variance and run Miller-style power analysis before fixing n.
- INFERENCE (nondeterminism): because temperature 0 is not deterministic, run K≥3 repeats per seed and average them before the paired tests. For local vLLM models, either enable batch-invariant or deterministic mode or send requests one at a time. Log exact model IDs or dates, the system-prompt hash and the tool schema version, because frontier API models change silently.
- INFERENCE (real-world protocol): build 2–4 arena layouts with tape-measured tag poses, and give each layout a matched Gazebo digital twin. Obtain ground truth from an overhead camera tracking a large AprilTag on top of the robot, using a tag ID range the robot never uses as a beacon; the HeRoSwarm result suggests about 1 cm on a table-sized field (unverified for larger arenas). Fix the start poses, interleave models in a Latin-square order within one session with a blind operator, and log battery voltage, since 4×AA sag affects motion. Then report sim–real agreement per model with MMRV and Pearson. Only if the rankings agree (low MMRV) can the large sim comparison be said to predict real performance.
- INFERENCE (effect on D1 feasibility and risk): published spatial-reasoning benchmarks (PPNL, Lost in Aggregation, CubeBench) show LLMs failing at long-horizon sequential spatial tracking. Expect raw-context LLM localisation to drift far worse than an EKF over long runs. The benchmark stays scientifically meaningful, but the headline finding may be 'which scaffolding lets an LLM approach a classical estimator', not 'LLMs do SLAM'. Frame the hypotheses that way before collecting data.

CHALLENGES:
- The Isaac Sim tooling implication (and the verdict above) relies on a misread release note. The 5.1 notes FIXED CameraInfo so that it no longer always sets fy = fx. Also, 5.1 has been superseded by Isaac Sim 6.0 (general availability announced 8 Jun 2026). The recommendation should say: evaluate 6.0 on aarch64 before committing to the unsupported 5.1 build. The 'which release supersedes 5.1' unknown is answerable.
- Implication 1 ('ATE after SE(2) Umeyama alignment with evo'): evo offers SE(3) or Sim(3) Umeyama plus --project_to_plane xy, not a native SE(2) alignment. For planar data this is workable, but it should be stated as project-to-plane followed by SE(3) alignment, or implemented as a custom SE(2) Umeyama. Also, aligning the LLM's pose belief frees the evaluation from the arbitrary map frame. That suits SLAM, but it hides a systematic heading bias that a single rigid transform can absorb. RPE and tag-map error must accompany ATE.
- The loop-closure metric leans on Schubert et al. Their claim that true positives matter more than false positives is explicitly conditional on a robust graph back end. In an LLM-as-SLAM system there is no robust optimiser: one hallucinated revisit can corrupt the whole map. Loop-closure precision should therefore be weighted at least as heavily as recall, the opposite of what that citation suggests.
- The tag-map RMSE 'valid because tag IDs give data association for free' holds only if the detector reads IDs correctly. With the ESP32-CAM at low resolution, ID misreads or the VLM hallucinating IDs break that assumption. Kümmerle et al. actually recommend trajectory-based evaluation over landmark-based evaluation for EKF-type methods. Tag-ID precision and recall (which the researcher includes) must gate the map RMSE.
- The statistics transferred from STEP, SAVI (Snyder 2026), SIMPLER/MMRV, Kress-Gazit and Shuo Wang 2026 were all developed for manipulation policies, mostly with binary success. Their applicability to navigation or SLAM with continuous ATE is inference. MMRV in particular needs several models evaluated in real episodes to be meaningful; with 2–3 real models it has very little resolving power.
- Episode-count arithmetic: the 2/2^n floor is correct for exact Wilcoxon or sign tests with no zero differences. SciPy's default zero_method='wilcox' drops ties, though, and exact mode is used only without ties or zeros (n ≤ 13 otherwise uses permutation). For McNemar, the floor depends on the number of DISCORDANT pairs, not on n. Two models that both succeed (or both fail) on most episodes need far more than 10 real episodes. The '≥10 paired episodes' rule is therefore a lower bound, not a sufficient number.
- The analysis plan ('Friedman, then pairwise Wilcoxon with Holm') is defensible, but Demšar's own recommendation is Friedman with mean-rank post-hoc tests (Nemenyi and similar). Pairwise Wilcoxon is justified by Benavoli et al. 2016, which the researcher did not cite. Note too that statsmodels multipletests defaults to 'hs', not 'holm'.
- Overhead-camera ground truth '~1 cm' comes from HeRoSwarm's static calibration of tags placed on marked points, not from tracking a moving robot. For a moving TT-motor car seen through a webcam, motion blur, latency relative to the robot's timestamps, and the parallax of the tag's height must be measured. The error budget is unverified, not '~1 cm'.
- 'Isaac Sim Replicator randomisation' for benchmark worlds: the cited tutorial is a pipeline for synthetic perception images, not an evaluation harness, so reusing it for seeded navigation worlds is extra engineering.
