SUMMARY: The manipulation VLAs with open weights (OpenVLA 7B, pi0/pi0-FAST/pi0.5 through openpi, SmolVLA 450M, NVIDIA GR00T N1.x at 3B, Octo 27M/93M) map camera images and a language instruction straight to low-level action chunks for arms or humanoids. RT-2/RT-2-X are described as closed. No source claims any of these works on a new embodiment without fine-tuning: OpenVLA says it "only works well on domains from the training dataset", openpi says pi0 "may or may not work for you", and GR00T supports zero-shot only on its pretraining embodiments. The only one that drives a base is pi0.5, which commands base velocities for a holonomic mobile manipulator, and its authors list multi-room navigation as future work. Navigation foundation models are a closer fit. GNM, ViNT and NoMaD are 19-31M-parameter RGB policies that output normalized waypoints, and NoMaD adds goal-free exploration. CrossFormer and OmniVLA are cross-embodiment or omni-goal VLAs. X-Mobility sends velocity commands to a differential-drive Nova Carter with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer. NaVILA, NaVid, Uni-NaVid, NavFoM and InternVLA-N1 are 7-8B video-VLM navigators that output language moves such as "moving forward 75cm", discrete FORWARD 25 cm / TURN 30° actions, or trajectories. None of these sources describes a model that estimates metric pose or builds a landmark map. ViNT's own robots use wheel odometry or GPS "to track their position", OmniVLA takes a GPS pose, X-Mobility's ROS 2 demo needs a map and a clicked 2D Goal Pose, GemNav gets its pose from LiDAR-inertial SLAM, and NaVid simply works "without any maps, odometers, or depth inputs". The one mapping-like ability is topological: GNM, ViNT and NoMaD keep a graph of past camera images as nodes and localize by finding "the node with smallest temporal distance". This is appearance-based place recognition, a fair baseline for the revisit (loop-closure) sub-task but not for metric SLAM. There is evidence of running on aarch64: GR00T N1.7 officially supports DGX Spark (CUDA 13.0), a community project ran pi0.5 on a DGX Spark at about 95 ms with TensorRT FP8+NVFP4, a GR00T N1.5 fine-tuning guide for Spark lists aarch64 workarounds, NVIDIA has a pi0.5 tutorial for Jetson Thor, OpenVLA INT4 runs on a Jetson AGX Orin at about 375 ms, and NoMaD/ViNT were deployed on Jetson Orin. Overall, putting VLAs or navigation models on a SLAM leaderboard would compare unlike things. They should enter the benchmark through a shared bounded-move action interface, as exploration or navigation policies and as place-recognition baselines, and be reported as N/A on pose and map accuracy.

IMPLICATIONS:
- INFERENCE: Manipulation VLAs (OpenVLA, pi0/pi0.5, SmolVLA, GR00T N1.x, Octo) do not belong on a SLAM leaderboard. None estimates pose or a landmark map, and none has been shown to drive an unseen differential-drive car without teleop data and fine-tuning. Either leave them out, or add one (pi0.5 or SmolVLA via LeRobot) as an optional 'fine-tuned end-to-end explorer', stating clearly that it was fine-tuned on this car and needs the geometry from a separate estimator.
- INFERENCE: Navigation foundation models enter at two points. (a) As the exploration/navigation policy that picks the next move. (b) As appearance-based place-recognition baselines for revisit detection: GNM/ViNT/NoMaD's temporal-distance head finds 'the node with smallest temporal distance'. They should get 'N/A' on metric pose error and landmark-map error, because in every source they consume odometry, GPS, or a prior map and do not produce one.
- INFERENCE / design suggestion: make the benchmark capability-decomposed, with a shared I/O contract. T1 (estimation): replay identical logged episodes (JPEG frames, encoder odometry, IMU yaw, AprilTag detections) to every LLM/VLM agent and to a classical landmark-SLAM reference; score robot pose and tag-map error. T2 (loop closure): given a frame plus history, is this a revisit? Compare LLM/VLM against the NoMaD/ViNT distance head and a tag-ID oracle. T3 (exploration policy): every model chooses among the same bounded moves, localized by a common classical back end so only the policy differs; score tags found per metre, coverage, collisions and path length. T4 (full LLM-as-SLAM closed loop): LLM/VLM agents only, plus a hybrid where NoMaD explores and the LLM maps.
- INFERENCE: The student's bounded high-level move API lines up with published navigation outputs: Uni-NaVid's FORWARD 25 cm / TURN 30°, NaVILA's 'moving forward 75cm', and GNM/ViNT/NoMaD waypoints that a PD controller already converts to velocities. Adapters can therefore quantize waypoints or trajectories into the same move set the LLM uses. That keeps every model out of the motor loop and makes the comparison fair.
- INFERENCE: Run the robot stop-and-go: it waits for each decision, then executes a bounded move. Report wall-clock latency separately. This stops API-model latency from confounding accuracy, and allows exact replay in simulation. The latencies found span roughly 50 ms to 1 s: pi0.5 about 95 ms on a Spark, OpenVLA INT4 about 375 ms on Orin, ViNT at 4 Hz, Uni-NaVid about 5 Hz on an A100.
- INFERENCE / risk: GNM/ViNT/NoMaD were trained on and recommend a wide-angle fisheye camera, while the ESP32-CAM OV3660 is a low-mounted, probably narrower camera sending JPEG over Wi-Fi. Zero-shot navigation performance will likely drop. Fit a wide-angle lens, or record in Isaac Sim/Gazebo a camera model that matches the real one, and treat the real-world numbers as a sim-to-real stress test (the VLN survey found 61% to 22% for monolithic RGB-only models).
- INFERENCE: Models that need depth (InternVLA-N1's NavDP variant) or LiDAR/GPS pose (GemNav, OmniVLA's pose goals, X-Mobility's map-based goals) do not fit the fixed hardware, which has no range sensor and no localization in an unknown map. Good real-robot candidates are RGB-only NoMaD/ViNT/GNM (small, easy on the Spark), and possibly Uni-NaVid or NaVILA (7-8B, fit in 121 GB unified memory).
- INFERENCE: On the DGX Spark, GR00T N1.7 has official support and pi0.5 has community proof of running, but openpi officially supports only Ubuntu 22.04, and GR00T N1.5 on Spark needed hand-built aarch64 dependencies. Plan time for dependency workarounds, and prefer the small PyTorch navigation models (NoMaD/ViNT) as the first navigation-model baseline.
- INFERENCE: X-Mobility already has an Isaac Sim + Carter + ROS 2 pipeline and outputs velocities for a differential drive, so it is a natural simulation-only navigation baseline, provided the classical localization back end supplies its route/goal. It cannot serve in an unknown-map localization track.
- INFERENCE: The literature (VLM-GLoc, GemNav, ViNT) consistently uses VLMs, VLAs and navigation models around a classical pose estimator, not in place of one. So the 'LLM as SLAM' claim should be tested against a hybrid ablation (LLM front-end, classical EKF or factor-graph back end) as well as the classical reference, and the writeup should say the pure-LLM estimator is the novel, unproven part.

CHALLENGES:
- Tag-ID loop closure is trivial. AprilTag 36h11 IDs are unique, so a 'tag-ID oracle' solves revisit detection almost perfectly whenever a tag is visible. T2 (loop closure) only discriminates between models if it is scored on tag-free revisits or with deliberately duplicated or aliased tag IDs. Otherwise every model will look good. No source addresses this; it is a design flaw in the proposed T2.
- Using the NoMaD/ViNT/GNM temporal-distance head as a loop-closure detector is off-label. The papers use it to associate the current observation with nodes of a graph from a prior or ongoing traversal ('finds the node with smallest temporal distance'). No source evaluates it as a place-recognition detector with precision/recall. Present it as an unvalidated baseline, and add a standard global-descriptor place-recognition baseline; Mobility VLA's hierarchical localizer uses a k-NN global image descriptor.
- 'N/A on pose and map error' is right for the models as released, but the literature does show hybrids that get metric pose from vision. Mobility VLA localizes by PnP against a COLMAP SfM map (Appendix 7.1). So the classical reference should be stated precisely (e.g. tag-landmark EKF/factor graph with wheel+IMU odometry). 'Consistently uses classical estimators' rests on 3-4 examples (VLM-GLoc, GemNav, ViNT, Mobility VLA). That is suggestive, not a survey-level finding.
- Stop-and-go with quantized moves is a distribution shift for GNM/ViNT/NoMaD. They take a fixed-rate temporal context of past frames (ViNT runs at 4 Hz with a PD tracker, receding horizon), and their waypoints are normalized by robot top speed. Quantizing their waypoints into discrete FORWARD/TURN moves and feeding them frames spaced by bounded moves is an untested adapter, so 'fair comparison' is asserted, not shown. By contrast, Uni-NaVid/NaVid were trained on exactly 25 cm / 30° steps ('consistent with all training navigation data'). The shared move set should therefore be 25 cm / 30° so that at least those models run in-distribution.
- Recommending Uni-NaVid or NaVILA as 'good real-robot candidates' is a leap. Both are instruction-following VLN models, not exploration or mapping models. NaVILA's mid-level language commands were executed by a legged locomotion RL policy. No source shows either on aarch64 or a DGX Spark. Uni-NaVid's 5 Hz was on an A100, and NaVILA pins an old transformers 4.37.2 stack. Fitting in 121 GB of memory says nothing about whether the model runs or works on this camera.
- Applying the VLN survey's 61%->22% drop to NoMaD/ViNT on the ESP32-CAM is an extrapolation. The survey measures monolithic RGB-only VLN models on instruction following, not goal-free exploration policies. The camera-mismatch risk is real (fisheye recommended), but its size is unknown.
- Quoting pi0.5 at ~95 ms on Spark and GR00T N1.7 Spark support as benchmark-relevant is misleading. Those latencies are for manipulation checkpoints (pi05_libero / DROID), and no released checkpoint of either model can command a mobile base. Budget time for them only if the student will collect teleop data and fine-tune. Otherwise they should not enter the benchmark at all.
- The summary understates LLM/MLLM ability at low-level discrete navigation. EmbodiedBench's 28.9% figure is for manipulation; on EB-Navigation (discrete move/rotate actions) GPT-4o scored 57.7%. That supports feeding LLM agents the same bounded-move interface, and undercuts any framing that MLLMs are generally poor at atomic actions.
- X-Mobility as a sim baseline has an unflagged compatibility risk. The demo targets Isaac Sim 4.2 on Ubuntu 22.04 with ROS 2 Humble, and TensorRT engines must be rebuilt for the target GPU. The student has Isaac Sim 5.1 built from source on aarch64. No source confirms X-Mobility works on that stack, and it needs a route from robot position, so it depends entirely on the classical localizer.
- The spark-projects pi0.5 figure (~94.7 ms) claims to 'match the Jetson Thor reference (~94 ms)', but NVIDIA's official Thor page reports ~49 ms (FP8+NVFP4) and ~132 ms BF16. Treat the community number as unverified, not as proof of parity.

MISSED:
- Mobility VLA gets metric robot pose from classical vision, not the VLM. 'We use COLMAP, an off-the-shelf structure-from-motion pipeline to estimate the pose of the robot for each frame in the tour... 3D point landmarks' and 'the pose of O is computed by solving the corresponding Perspective-n-Point problem'. This is a direct precedent for 'VLM for semantics, classical geometry for pose'. https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07775
- OmniVLA-edge size is known, which resolves one of the researcher's unknowns. Table VI: 'OmniVLA-edge CLIP EfficientNet-B0 50M' vs 'OmniVLA Llama2-7B DINOv2+SigLIP 7.5B'. OmniVLA-edge is 'based on prior single-modality vision-based navigation policies such as ViNT, NoMaD'. https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19480
- EmbodiedBench EB-Navigation: best model GPT-4o at 57.7% with discrete move/rotate actions, and 'disabling vision causes GPT-4o's EB-Navigation performance to drop sharply from 57.7% to 17.4%'. This is directly relevant evidence that frontier MLLMs can drive via bounded moves. https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09560
- NavFoM paper vs project page conflict: the paper says 'For real-world deployment, we employ a remote server equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU', while the page says RTX 5090. The LLM backbone is Qwen2-7B. https://arxiv.org/html/2509.12129
- GNM's training data includes differential-drive robots: 'a TurtleBot is differential-drive, expects low-level velocity commands, and has a top speed of 0.5m/s'. The 6 robots include TurtleBot, Jackal, Warthog, Spot, an ATV and an RC car, which makes it the closest embodiment match to a small diff-drive car. https://arxiv.org/html/2210.03370
- ViNT's goal-directed exploration uses a heuristic that 'may be geometric (e.g., Euclidean distance)', which requires the robot's position, and 'the robot incrementally builds M while searching for the goal'. https://arxiv.org/html/2306.14846
- OpenVLA's README says 'OpenVLA typically requires fine-tuning on a small demonstration dataset (~100 demos) from your target domain robot'. That quantifies the teleop cost if a manipulation VLA were adapted. https://github.com/openvla/openvla
- GR00T N1.7's pretrain tags include REAL_R1_PRO_SHARPA as well as REAL_G1, both relative-EEF. Inference needs '16 GB+ VRAM' and access to the gated nvidia/Cosmos-Reason2-2B backbone. The CUDA 13 platforms (Thor/Spark) need scripts/patch_triton_cuda13.sh. https://github.com/NVIDIA/Isaac-GR00T
- VLN survey: hierarchical framework 'achieves a higher real-world success rate of 51%'. The paper's full title is 'A Comprehensive Survey and Systematic Real-World Evaluation of Embodied Vision-and-Language Navigation' (Liuyi Wang et al., submitted July 9, 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09792
- The official visualnav-transformer deployment is ROS 1 Noetic 'tested on a LoCoBot running Ubuntu 20.04'. The ROS 2 path on the Spark therefore depends on community forks (TohokuQuix, RobotecAI), neither of which documents an aarch64/arm64 image. https://github.com/robodhruv/visualnav-transformer and https://github.com/TohokuQuix/visualnav-transformer-ros2
- CrossFormer's navigation deployment reuses 'the graph-based planner and distance function from Shah et al.', so its localization within the topological map comes from GNM's distance head, not from CrossFormer itself. https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11812

- No direct evidence found that GNM/ViNT/NoMaD, NaVILA, Uni-NaVid, NavFoM or InternVLA-N1 have run on aarch64 or a DGX Spark. NoMaD/ViNT evidence covers Jetson Orin only; the 7-8B navigation VLMs were run on x86 A100 or RTX 5090 servers.

- Whether NavFoM weights are publicly released; the project page links no code or weights.
- OmniVLA-edge parameter count and inference compute; exact inference memory for NaVILA-8B and Uni-NaVid-7B.
- RT-2 weight status confirmed from a primary Google/DeepMind source; only OpenVLA's description of RT-2-X as 'closed' was seen.
- Whether any VLA has been fine-tuned or evaluated on a small differential-drive toy car. Community SmolVLA checkpoints exist for the holonomic LeKiwi base, but their base action format was not confirmed.

- The formal definitions of navigation metrics (SPL etc.) were not fetched: the Anderson et al. 2018 (arXiv:1807.06757) abstract page did not include them.
