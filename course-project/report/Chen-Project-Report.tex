\begin{abstract}
BeaconMind asks whether a large language model (LLM), rather than a classical estimator, can keep a robot's pose
and landmark map, the state of simultaneous localization and mapping (SLAM). A small differential-drive car explores an arena whose only landmarks are AprilTags with repeated IDs.
At every stop a stateless ReAct agent reads the odometry and tag detections as text, calls deterministic geometry
tools, and decides which detections to trust and match and which pose and map to report. In a
pre-registered study, three models replayed identical simulated logs of \bmnum{n.seeds} held-out seeds with a
2\,\% odometry over-read, against dead reckoning, a factor-graph reference and the same procedure written as code.
Claude Opus~5.5 and Qwen3.8-Flash-Next tracked the car better than dead reckoning (trajectory error lower by
\bmnum{h1.opus.gain} and \bmnum{h1.qwen.gain}\,cm; Holm-adjusted $p=\bmnum{h1.opus.p-holm}$ and
$\bmnum{h1.qwen.p-holm}$), but neither interval lay wholly below $-$\bmnum{sesoi.cm}\,cm, the smallest difference that
matters, and both largely reproduced the coded procedure. Gemma~4 26B was worse than dead reckoning on
\bmnum{h1.gemma.worse} seeds, mostly on steps answered without a tool (post hoc). On a real car the coded procedure
drove \bmnum{real.n} episodes in a carpeted room; replayed on their logs and scored against a tag resection at the end
of \bmnum{real.n-circle} of them, the median final-position error was \bmnum{real.b0.median}\,cm for dead reckoning,
\bmnum{real.b2.median}\,cm for the coded procedure, \bmnum{real.opus.median}\,cm for Opus~5.5,
\bmnum{real.qwen.median}\,cm for Qwen3.8 and \bmnum{real.gemma.median}\,cm for Gemma~4. The car's odometry drifted far more than the simulator's.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Agentic language models now drive robots through tools: they decompose instructions into admissible
skills~\cite{ahn2022saycan}, close the loop with textual feedback~\cite{huang2023inner}, and interleave
reasoning with actions~\cite{yao2023react}, and frameworks such as ROSA expose the Robot Operating System
(ROS) to such agents~\cite{royce2025rosa}. These systems take the robot's state, its pose and its map, as given
or from a classical estimator. Diagnostics of LLM spatial reasoning explain the caution: models
treat spatial questions in isolation~\cite{li2026llms}, collapse on long whole-route plans~\cite{jiang2026lost},
and make arithmetic mistakes even when their decomposition is right~\cite{gao2023pal}. Tools remove the
arithmetic; what remains open is whether the \emph{decisions} of an estimator, which detection to trust,
which landmark a detection belongs to, and whether a pose fix or a loop closure is accepted, can be owned
by a language model. If they can, an agent can explain, question and refuse on the basis of its own state;
if not, the boundary of agentic control is clearer.

\textbf{Goal.} BeaconMind's ReAct agent must keep its own pose belief and AprilTag map on a small car well
enough to explore a walled arena, return to its start and drive to a named beacon, while a deterministic
validator keeps every move bounded and safe. Tag IDs may repeat, so the tag code does not settle data association.
Of the three missions only exploration was evaluated, in replay and in a few closed-loop episodes; returning to the
start and driving to a named beacon were not run (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\textbf{Contributions.} (1)~A tool-grounded, stateless ReAct agent design in which the LLM owns every SLAM
decision, single-timestep tools compute every geometric quantity, and a validator below the model enforces
safety (Section~\ref{sec:system}); the code is public, except the car's firmware (Section~\ref{sec:system}). (2)~A pre-registered evaluation protocol that replays
identical logs through every arm, compares the agent with dead reckoning, a factor-graph reference and the
procedure written as code, measures tool-call F1 against that code, and scores the agent's notes with a rubric whose LLM judge is
calibrated without hand labels
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). (3)~Results for three models on \bmnum{n.seeds} held-out simulated episodes: two
models beat dead reckoning by a statistically supported but practically small margin and add little beyond the
coded procedure, and one fails for a reason a post hoc check identifies (Section~\ref{sec:results}). (4)~A replay of dead
reckoning, the coded procedure, Opus~5.5, Qwen3.8 and Gemma~4 on \bmnum{real.n} episodes recorded
on the real car in a surveyed room, scored against a tag resection at the end of \bmnum{real.n-circle} of them, and
what the car showed that the simulator did not (Sections~\ref{sec:real} and~\ref{sec:sim2real}).

\textbf{Research questions.} RQ1 (primary): replayed on held-out logs, is the agent's pose estimate more
accurate than dead reckoning, and is it within twice the error of a factor-graph reference? RQ2: how
precisely does the agent associate detections when tag IDs repeat, and how faithfully does it execute the
procedure? RQ3: in closed loop, in simulation and on the car, does it complete its missions? RQ4: do the
three models differ?

\textbf{Roadmap.} Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews related work, Section~\ref{sec:system} describes the
system, Section~\ref{sec:experiments} the experiments as pre-registered and run, Section~\ref{sec:results}
the results and their limits, and Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.

\section{Related Works}\label{sec:related}
\subsection{Language-Conditioned Planning and Closed-Loop Agents}
LLM plans for household tasks are plausible but often not admissible~\cite{huang2022zeroshot}; SayCan grounded
each skill with a learned affordance value, without which planning success fell from 84\% to
67\%~\cite{ahn2022saycan}. Inner Monologue showed that closed-loop textual feedback significantly improves
completion~\cite{huang2023inner}, and ReAct's interleaving of reasoning, action and observation beat imitation
and reinforcement learning on ALFWorld and WebShop by 34 and 10 points~\cite{yao2023react}. Kambhampati
\textit{et~al.} argue that LLMs can neither plan nor verify plans alone and should propose candidates that sound
external verifiers check~\cite{kambhampati2024llmmodulo}. ROSA packages a ReAct agent with ROS
tools~\cite{royce2025rosa}, an agentic interface in the classification of Salimpour
\textit{et~al.}~\cite{salimpour2025towards}. Failures remain: in the Embodied Agent Interface, trajectory-feasibility
errors are common (45.2\%)~\cite{li2024embodied}, and the most safety-conscious agent of
SafeAgentBench rejects only 10\% of detailed hazardous tasks~\cite{yin2025safeagentbench}. BeaconMind adopts one
admissible move per observation, a closed loop, and, following LLM-Modulo, a deterministic validator between the
model and the motors. It differs in what the model reasons about: these works plan tasks and take the robot's
state as given, while BeaconMind asks the model to estimate that state.

\subsection{Language Models in Navigation, Mapping, and SLAM}
In the language-driven navigation systems reviewed here the geometry stays classical (Table~\ref{tab:division}):
the pose comes from odometry, SLAM or a pre-built scene graph, and the model plans over the graph, scores or selects
frontiers, or picks goals~\cite{rana2023sayplan,yokoyama2024vlfm,yu2023l3mvn,lee2026spatial}. The closest system
identified in the literature search, by Syarubany \textit{et~al.}, issues one constrained command per junction
with AprilTags as anchors but takes its global pose from ORB-SLAM3~\cite{syarubany2026llm}.
Where language models come closer to SLAM they supply semantics or discrete choices: SEO-SLAM's agents verify,
relabel and merge object landmarks while association is a linear assignment and the map comes from a factor
graph~\cite{hong2025seoslam}; Lyu \textit{et~al.} let a multimodal LLM choose among place-recognition
candidates~\cite{lyu2024tell}; and Sandfuchs \textit{et~al.} state of their gridworld controllers that
``this is not SLAM: the uncertainty is in the map, not in pose''~\cite{sandfuchs2026llms}. Learned policies
such as NoMaD avoid metric estimation altogether~\cite{sridhar2024nomad}. In none of these systems is the pose
estimate in the model's hands, and to my knowledge no prior study reports a trajectory or map error for a
landmark-SLAM estimate maintained by an LLM; that measurement is the gap this project targets.

\subsection{Limits of LLM Spatial Reasoning and the Case for Tools}
Jiang \textit{et~al.} find that whole-route maze navigation by GPT-4o and others falls to near-zero success by
$10\times10$ mazes, that structured coordinate text is the most navigable input, and that handing corridor
walking and dead-end retreat to a deterministic walker, with the model queried only at junctions through an
explicit cell-type prompt, lifts GPT-4o's success by up to 92 points at mid sizes, although the collapse returns by
$30\times30$~\cite{jiang2026lost}. Li \textit{et~al.} show that accuracy depends on the representation:
Gemini-2.5-Flash with chain-of-thought prompting reaches 80--86\% on small mazes given as adjacency lists but
16--34\% on character grids~\cite{li2026llms}. Deng \textit{et~al.} find that the best vision-language
model reaches a macro-F1 of 0.66 on relative camera pose, against 0.99 for a geometric pipeline~\cite{deng2026lost},
and PAL shows that LLMs decompose problems well but make arithmetic mistakes, and that off-loading the solution
step to an interpreter is more accurate than much larger models~\cite{gao2023pal}. BeaconMind follows these
findings: detections arrive as structured text, every geometric quantity comes from a tool restricted to one
timestep, the system prompt prescribes a per-step procedure (predict, associate, correct, update the map, check
loop closure, decide), and each step is posed as an isolated decision, which Jiang \textit{et~al.} found more
reliable than the same decision inside a long plan.

\subsection{Fiducial Landmark SLAM and Evaluation Practice}
AprilTag gives a tag's identity and full six-degree-of-freedom pose from one image~\cite{olson2011apriltag}; TagSLAM builds a factor
graph over tags and notes that ``loop closure is guaranteed once a tag is successfully
decoded''~\cite{pfrommer2019tagslam}, and iSAM2 solves such graphs incrementally~\cite{kaess2012isam2}. Sturm
\textit{et~al.} formalize the absolute trajectory error (ATE) and relative pose error~\cite{sturm2012benchmark},
and Anderson \textit{et~al.} recommend an explicit DONE action and success weighted by path length
(SPL)~\cite{anderson2018evaluation}. BeaconMind uses a GTSAM/iSAM2 graph as its best-case reference, dead
reckoning as its floor, and removes the assumption that makes association trivial in TagSLAM: some IDs are
duplicated on purpose.

\subsection{Evaluating LLM Agents: Metrics, Judges, and Statistics}
HELM argues for reporting several metrics side by side instead of accuracy alone~\cite{liang2023holistic}, which
BeaconMind follows with pose, map, association, tool-use, text-quality and cost metrics. Tool calls are scored in
the spirit of Gorilla and the Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard, which match the structure and arguments of
each call against a reference call~\cite{patil2024gorilla,patil2025bfcl}. Free text is scored by an
LLM judge: Zheng \textit{et~al.} found that a strong judge agrees with human experts about as often as humans
agree with each other (85\% versus 81\% on MT-Bench expert votes without ties) and named position and verbosity
biases~\cite{zheng2023judging};
Panickssery \textit{et~al.} showed that LLM evaluators recognize and favor their own
generations~\cite{panickssery2024llm}; G-Eval has the judge reason before filling a scoring
form~\cite{liu2023geval}. Two works shape the automated calibration used here: CheckList's invariance and
directional-expectation tests~\cite{ribeiro2020beyond}, and Doddapaneni \textit{et~al.}, whose targeted
perturbations showed that evaluator LLMs missed quality drops in over half of the cases~\cite{doddapaneni2024finding}.
For statistics, Dror \textit{et~al.} give a protocol for choosing significance tests and note that the
Wilcoxon signed-rank test~\cite{wilcoxon1945individual} is applicable to most NLP setups~\cite{dror2018hitchhikers}; Holm's
procedure controls the family-wise error over several hypotheses~\cite{holm1979simple}; the bootstrap gives
confidence intervals~\cite{efron1993introduction}; the rank-biserial correlation is the matching effect
size~\cite{kerby2014simple}; and weighted kappa measures agreement on ordered levels~\cite{cohen1968weighted}.

\textbf{One step.} The car moves only in five bounded moves (0.25\,m or 0.10\,m forward, 0.10\,m back, $\pm30^\circ$
turns) and stops after each. At every stop the harness sends the agent a step message: the mission, an
operator request (usually none), the \emph{observation} (the odometry increment since the last stop, the tag
detections with ID, range, bearing and quality, harness counters, and safety flags, which come from the validator in closed loop and from the simulator in
the study),
and the agent's own previous report, which is its only memory. The agent runs a ReAct loop: it reasons, calls
tools (Table~\ref{tab:tools}), reads their results and repeats, then returns one JSON report with its pose,
the source of that pose (beacon fix, relocalization, position fix, kept prior or odometry), its associations, loop closures,
map, bookkeeping memory, decision and a one-sentence \emph{note} for the operator. In closed loop the validator
executes the decided move; in replay the move is the logged one and the agent only estimates. The study of
this report ran in replay, so the agent's move decisions and the validator were not exercised.

\input{tab_tools}

\textbf{Who decides what.} The system prompt (v3.3; 4,352 tokens by the Qwen tokenizer; developed and smoke-tested with Qwen3.5, not an arm of the study)
states a per-step procedure: predict the pose
from odometry; gate each detection against the map entries with the same ID; correct the pose from matched,
confirmed beacons; add new beacons as tentative and confirm them after two sightings over a 0.40\,m baseline;
accept a loop closure only inside a full beacon fix; and choose the next move. The tools return every number
and flag; the agent decides which results to accept and writes the state. When two physical tags share an ID,
a detection far from the existing beacon of that ID becomes a second beacon labeled \texttt{B<id>b}. The same
procedure written as Python code is the baseline B2, the \emph{coded procedure}, except one clause: rule~2's
zeroing of a move whose odometry is flagged as slipping (Section~\ref{sec:real}). The clause is in the study's prompt
too, but the simulator never sets the flag, so the study's fidelity numbers are unaffected; on the car it helped in
the one case where no fix followed at once.

\textbf{Safety.} The design has three layers: an out-of-band STOP that aborts any move and sets a persistent
latch; the validator of Table~\ref{tab:tools} together with the Pico's watchdog and stall detection; and the
agent's own guardrails (never move on an error, refuse requests outside the missions, ask when a target is
ambiguous). The car runs the author's build of the course's lab firmware, which has a watchdog, a deadman and
a stall cut-off (the project firmware was dropped); the bounded moves are executed and stopped from the host. That
firmware boots armed, and after a Pico reset the motor driver's inputs float for about 1.5\,s, so operator
procedures (Section~\ref{sec:procedure}) replace the design's disarmed boot. Checked on the car on 2026-11-25 in
single trials (\path{evidence/M3/M3.T13/smoke.md}, \texttt{evidence/M3/M3.T6/}; the STOP key and the Ctrl+C were
sent through the terminal multiplexer by the agent running the session, not typed at a keyboard): the STOP key
during a forward move set the latch and zeroed the motor duty within \bmnum{stop.duty-s}\,s, and the wheels stood
still within \bmnum{stop.still-s}\,s on the stand and \bmnum{stop.floor-s}\,s on the floor (limit
\bmnum{stop.max-s}\,s); as far as the logs show, no move ran while the latch was set, and one ran again after the
operator cleared it; killing the host process mid-move stopped the wheels by the car's own command timeout. For unattended runs a batch runner on the host adds
a footprint check against the room's driving area before every move and software halts (Section~\ref{sec:procedure}).

\textbf{Runtimes.} The benchmark uses a minimal harness with only the nine tools, because ROSA (1.0.10) adds its
own 47 tools and nine fixed prompts to every call, about 6,000 more input tokens, as counted in
\path{AGENT-DESIGN.md}. ROSA was planned as the demonstration runtime but was not used: the author chose to build the
demonstration video from the harness's own logs, which show the same agent, prompt and tools.

\subsection{Research Questions and Hypotheses}\label{sec:rq}
The arms are B0 (dead reckoning), B1 (a factor-graph reference), B2 (the coded procedure) and L2 (the LLM
agent, with three models); Table~\ref{tab:config} lists them. The unit of analysis is the seed, and the
primary endpoint is the absolute trajectory error (ATE-RMSE, Section~\ref{sec:metrics}) of one episode.

\textbf{RQ1 (primary): estimation.} Replay on held-out simulated logs with duplicated tag IDs and a 2\,\%
systematic odometry over-read. The pilot (Section~\ref{sec:pilot}) showed that unbiased odometry leaves no room
to improve on dead reckoning in a 2.25\,m arena, so the study uses the drift condition only.
\emph{H1, one per model (family~1, primary):} $H_0$: the median per-seed paired difference
$\mathrm{ATE}_{\mathrm{L2}}-\mathrm{ATE}_{\mathrm{B0}}$ is $\geq0$; $H_1$: it is $<0$; one-sided. The smallest
difference that matters is \bmnum{sesoi.cm}\,cm, one tenth of the 0.30\,m association gate: a smaller change
is unlikely to alter an association or safety decision. H1 is \emph{supported in practice} only if it is rejected
and the 95\,\% interval of the difference lies wholly below $-$\bmnum{sesoi.cm}\,cm; otherwise a rejected H1 is
reported as statistically but not practically supported.
\emph{H2, one per model (family~2, secondary):} $H_0$: the median per-seed
$\log(\mathrm{ATE}_{\mathrm{L2}}/\mathrm{ATE}_{\mathrm{B1}})-\log 2$ is $\geq0$; $H_1$: it is $<0$; one-sided.
The margin of twice the reference was set in the project plan before any data.

\textbf{RQ2 (descriptive): association and fidelity.} Association precision against the plan's target of
0.90; map quality; tool-call F1 and pose-source agreement with the coded procedure.
\textbf{RQ3 (descriptive): closed loop.} The first version's test H3 of navigation success
against B2 needed a closed-loop sweep, which was cut when the project was re-scoped, so H3 is not tested; a few
closed-loop episodes will be reported descriptively (Section~\ref{sec:real}).
\textbf{RQ4 (secondary, own family): model differences.} \emph{H4:} $H_0$: the three models' per-seed ATE
have the same distribution; $H_1$: at least one differs.

All tests use a family-wise $\alpha=\bmnum{alpha}$. Family~1 is tested with Holm's
procedure~\cite{holm1979simple}. Family~2 is tested, also with Holm, only if \emph{every} H1 is rejected (serial
gatekeeping); otherwise it is reported as not tested. Development data predicted that H2 would fail, and the
gate keeps a secondary hypothesis from taking power from the primary family. Everything else is descriptive.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
\textbf{Hardware.} The car is described in Section~\ref{sec:system}. Measured: mass 0.660\,kg, wheel
diameter 67\,mm, track 133.5\,mm, camera lens
\bmnum{cam.x}\,m ahead of the axle and \bmnum{cam.z}\,m above the floor. The camera was calibrated on
2026-11-18 at $\bmnum{cam.width}\times\bmnum{cam.height}$ pixels, JPEG quality \bmnum{cam.quality}
(\bmnum{cam.fps} frames/s over Wi-Fi), from \bmnum{cam.views} views of a printed checkerboard ($9\times6$ inner corners) on a rigid backing: focal lengths \bmnum{cam.fx} and \bmnum{cam.fy} pixels, principal point (\bmnum{cam.cx},
\bmnum{cam.cy}), reprojection error \bmnum{cam.rms} pixels. The horizontal field of view is
\bmnum{cam.hfov}$^\circ$ and the vertical \bmnum{cam.vfov}$^\circ$; the design and the simulator assumed
60$^\circ$. Two checks against a tape measure at about 1.04\,m gave distance errors of
\bmnum{cam.check1.err}\,\% and \bmnum{cam.check2.err}\,\%. The mount angles are roll \bmnum{cam.roll}$^\circ$,
pitch \bmnum{cam.pitch}$^\circ$ (nose up) and yaw \bmnum{cam.yaw}$^\circ$, each
$\pm$\bmnum{cam.angle-unc}$^\circ$. The author accepted three deviations from the calibration plan: the board
covered 46\,\% of the image grid instead of 80\,\% (the calibration tool's one failed check), so distortion
near three image edges is extrapolated; the printed board was not checked with a ruler, its scale being
bounded by the two distance checks; and the roll exceeds the plan's 3$^\circ$ mount limit and is to be corrected in the tag geometry. Study code ran on the DGX Spark; Opus and the judge ran in their providers' clouds.

\subsection{Metrics and Ground Truth}\label{sec:metrics}
Every metric is reported on a 0--1 scale where possible, with the raw value alongside.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Pose (primary):} the absolute trajectory error~\cite{sturm2012benchmark} as a root-mean-square
(ATE-RMSE) over all steps,
$\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{T}\sum_t\lVert\hat p_t-p_t\rVert^2}$, in the map frame fixed at the start pose, with no
alignment fitted, every arm at report precision (0.01\,m, 0.1$^\circ$); score
$\max(0,1-\mathrm{ATE}/0.25\,\mathrm{m})$, where 0.25\,m is one forward move. Secondary: final position error
and heading RMSE.
\item \textbf{Map:} an entry is correct if a tag with the same ID lies within 0.30\,m (the association gate);
otherwise it is a phantom. Reported: RMSE over correct entries, ID precision and recall.
\item \textbf{Association:} a match to an existing beacon is a true positive if that beacon was created from
the same physical tag and a false positive otherwise; a detection of an already mapped tag that is not matched
is a false negative. Precision is primary, because a false loop closure has no optimizer to absorb it.
\item \textbf{Tool-call precision, recall, F1:} gold calls are the calls the coded procedure makes on the same
observation and previous report the agent received. A call matches a gold call if the tool name, the
detection index, the beacon label and the candidate or match sets are equal, the odometry arguments are equal, and
every pose argument agrees within 0.011\,m and 0.11$^\circ$ (reports round to 0.01\,m and 0.1$^\circ$);
matching is one-to-one, micro-averaged per seed.
\item \textbf{Validity and state:} an invalid report (unparseable, a non-numeric pose or a malformed map) is
replaced by the previous one, dead-reckoned (a \emph{fallback}, counted). A report
that stops one to three closing characters short is completed and counted as \emph{repaired}. The pose and
map are scored as the agent wrote them; disagreements with the step's tool results are counted as state
violations, and a claimed full fix that no \texttt{fix\_pose} result supports as an \emph{unverified fix claim}.
\item \textbf{Note rubric:} the one-sentence note has no single right answer, so it gets a rubric with levels
0--2. \emph{Faithful} (weight 0.40): 0~=~asserts an event that did not happen or the wrong pose source;
1~=~one detail wrong; 2~=~all supported. \emph{Coverage} (0.25): 0~=~omits two or more of the step's map
events; 1~=~omits one; 2~=~none. \emph{Uncertainty} (0.25): 0~=~does not say how the pose was obtained;
1~=~says how; 2~=~also gives a reason or quality figure. \emph{Concise} (0.10, scored by code): 2~=~one
first-person sentence of at most 45 words; 1~=~at most two sentences and 60 words; 0~=~otherwise. Score $\sum_i w_i g_i/2$;
faithfulness 0 gates the note to 0. The judge sees what the agent saw and did but never the ground truth, and
scores 10 randomly drawn valid-report steps per seed and arm.
\item \textbf{Cost:} latency per step (under shared-server load, so not a closed-loop latency), tokens and
US dollars.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Ground truth.} In simulation the true pose and the true tag behind every detection are recorded at every
step, so labels carry no noise beyond report rounding (0.005\,m, 0.05$^\circ$); gold tool calls come from code;
all are fixed by the frozen logs before any arm is scored. \textbf{On the car} the planned overhead camera was
dropped, so pose error is a \emph{final-position} error. Its primary truth is the \emph{end circle}: when an episode
ends, the car turns a full circle of twelve views where it stands, and a least-squares resection fits the tags it
sees (\bmnum{real.circle.tags.min}--\bmnum{real.circle.tags.max} per circle) to their surveyed positions, with the views
placed by the odometry's and gyro's turns. It does not go through the batch's tracker, but it is not independent of
the arms: it uses the same camera, detector, tags and world file as the coded procedure and the models (dead
reckoning shares none of them), its ranges carry the detector's short bias (the resection has no bias term), and
the error is taken in a map frame anchored on the episode's start circle, whose own error enters. Its accuracy is
estimated only from its residuals (RMS \bmnum{real.circle.rms.min}--\bmnum{real.circle.rms.max}\,cm); no tape reading
checks it. When the episode ended by DONE no move followed the last report, so the circle measures that pose. Its
heading is not used as a reference, because the views are placed by turns that slip. A secondary, tracker-dependent truth is
the batch's own tracked pose at the last step (odometry between tag fixes, blended by inverse variance); it sat
\bmnum{real.circle.off.min}--\bmnum{real.circle.off.max}\,cm from the end circles
(\path{evidence/M3/M3.T19/real-2026-11-25/README.md}). Neither truth resolves the \bmnum{sesoi.cm}\,cm
threshold; the real logs measure the sim-to-real gap descriptively and test no hypothesis.

\textbf{Judge calibration without hand labels.} The pipeline is automated by the author's choice, so the
judge is not calibrated against human scores. In the pilot, two candidate judges were checked on calibration
seeds 1001--1004 with \klNSol{} notes assembled by code with known defects, a false claim appended to 40
real notes, and a repeat run, reversed anchors and a padding sentence. The rule, fixed in code beforehand,
requires weighted $\kappa\geq0.6$~\cite{cohen1968weighted} and accuracy $\geq0.7$ on every judged criterion,
$\geq80$\,\% of false claims caught and $\geq90$\,\% of levels unchanged. GPT-6.1 Sol met every threshold
(lowest $\kappa$ \klKappaCoverSol{}) and was selected; Gemma~4 26B failed on coverage ($\kappa$
\klKappaCoverGemma{}). The calibration is thin in two places: the selected judge scored the known-label notes with a
wrong detail at the key's level on only \bmnum{judge.sol.wrong} of them, and on \bmnum{judge.inter.n} real notes the
two candidate judges agreed exactly on \bmnum{judge.inter.exact-faithful}\,\% of faithfulness levels but at $\kappa$
\bmnum{judge.inter.kappa-faithful} (nearly every note was faithful) and at $\kappa$ \bmnum{judge.inter.kappa-uncertainty}
on uncertainty (\path{evidence/M4/M4.T11/pilot_summary.json}).

\subsection{Experimental Procedure}\label{sec:procedure}
\textbf{Simulated replay, as run.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Record the logs: for each seed \bmnum{seed.first}--\bmnum{seed.last}, generate the world and let B2
drive one episode in the kinematic simulator until it reports DONE or reaches the 150-step budget. A seed on which B2 reaches an
unimplemented branch would be replaced by the next unused seed; none was. The logs hold
\bmnum{n.steps} steps (\bmnum{steps.min}--\bmnum{steps.max} per episode).
\item Freeze and verify the freeze; compute B0 and B1 with the frozen code (B2's estimates come with the log).
\item Replay every log through each LLM arm from a clean checkout, in a seed order shuffled with a recorded
random seed, \bmnum{workers.opus} seeds in parallel for Opus and Qwen3.8 and \bmnum{workers.gemma} for Gemma~4.
Opus has a spend cap of US\$\bmnum{cap.opus}.
\item Log every step as one JSON line: true pose, observation, previous report, every tool call and result,
the raw and parsed report, validity, fallback and repair flags, tokens, latency and cost.
\item Never drop a step silently. A model failure inside a step (an invalid report, the output cap, the
30-turn cap, a context overflow) is a counted fallback. An infrastructure failure (a server that still fails
after 7 attempts, or 3 for a client time-out of 3,600\,s) stops the seed without writing the step; the seed is
resumed once, and any step still missing would be scored as a fallback.
\item Verify the freeze again, run the judge on its sample (cap US\$\bmnum{cap.judge}) and run the frozen
analysis, which writes one row per seed and arm and every test.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Real car.} Two standing rules govern every real session. \emph{R1, sim first:} the session is first rehearsed in the kinematic simulator with the same room layout,
start pose, agent, prompt and tools, and compared with that rehearsal. \emph{R2, record everything:} a recorder writes one folder per
session with a ROS bag of every car topic, every camera frame, link and agent logs, every command, the agent's
traces and the tape sheet; the car is never driven without it. Only the one demonstration run is also filmed. The
procedure as run on 2026-11-25, with the author's approval for unattended batches
(\path{evidence/M3/M3.T12/batch-runner/README.md}):
\begin{enumerate}
\item Rehearse in simulation: the coded procedure in a simulator of the room (\bmnum{reh.n} episodes per
configuration, \path{evidence/M3/M3.T12/rehearsal-b2.md}), and before every batch a dry run of the batch runner
with the exact real arguments on a simulated car. The comparison with simulation is the aggregate one of
Section~\ref{sec:real}, not one per batch.
\item Check the car: a wheels-up stand test with the STOP check and a floor check of the encoder scale against the
wall tags, recorded in a pass marker that the batch runner requires. Power the Pico first and the motor battery
last; the motor driver's standby pull-down resistor was waived by the author.
\item Run a batch: the car starts on a taped pose or where the last batch left it; for each episode it drives to a
start pose and checks it with a full-turn resection, the coded procedure then chooses every move, and the episode
ends with the end circle. The motor battery stays on and nobody is at the car. Before every move the runner checks
the car's footprint against the driving area with a margin that grows with the tracked pose's uncertainty and
refuses a move that fails it; it halts the batch on a stall, lost telemetry, a STOP, or a tag survey or encoder
scale that the tags contradict.
\item Convert each episode that ended normally to the study's observation format and replay it through every arm,
scored against the end circle.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Statistical Analysis Plan}\label{sec:stats}
\textbf{Descriptive.} For every arm: median and mean over seeds with 95\,\% percentile-bootstrap intervals
(10,000 resamples)~\cite{efron1993introduction}, the range, the worst seed, and a plot of the per-seed points.
\textbf{Tests.} H1 and H2: the exact one-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the paired per-seed
values~\cite{wilcoxon1945individual}, chosen because the values are paired, few and not assumed
normal~\cite{dror2018hitchhikers}; zero differences are dropped and ties are handled exactly. The test assumes
differences symmetric about their median; if the adjusted skewness $G_1$ of the 30 values exceeds 1 in
absolute value, the exact sign test replaces it. The estimate reported with a signed-rank test is the
Hodges--Lehmann estimate (the median of the pairwise averages of the differences) with its exact 95\,\%
interval, and with a sign test the sample median with its exact interval. Also reported: the mean with a
bootstrap interval, the matched-pairs rank-biserial correlation~\cite{kerby2014simple} and Cohen's $d_z$. H4:
the Friedman test with Kendall's $W$, and then, only if it is significant, the three pairwise two-sided exact
signed-rank tests with Holm. A test that is not run counts as $p=1$. No outlier is removed; fallbacks stay in.
\textbf{Power.} The exact test was simulated at $n=30$ (4,000 simulations per scenario). For differences drawn from the pilot's effect (mean
$-2.70$\,cm, standard deviation 3.11\,cm) the probability of rejecting H1 at Holm's strictest level $\alpha/3$
is \bmnum{power.pilot.a3}; for an arm that merely reproduces the coded procedure it is \bmnum{power.proc.a3};
with the pilot's standard deviation at its 95\,\% upper bound it is \bmnum{power.sdhi.a3}. The probability of
\emph{practical} support was at most \bmnum{power.practical.max} in every scenario, since an interval wholly
below $-$\bmnum{sesoi.cm}\,cm needs a true effect near $-5$\,cm. The study was thus expected to detect an advantage over dead reckoning,
not a practically large one, and H2 was expected to fail.
\textbf{Held out.} The prompt was finished on 2026-09-27, before the study simulator and its seeds existed. Development used seeds 1000--1099; no LLM ran on the held-out seeds before the freeze. Partial runs of Qwen3.8 and Gemma~4 on development seeds set the output caps, the concurrency, the single run per seed and two parsing rules; no prompt, tool, threshold, metric or test was changed in response to any arm's
estimates in those runs.
\textbf{What would change my mind.} If an arm's ATE stays within 1\,cm of B2's on every seed, the model adds no
estimation value beyond the code it executes; if it does not beat B0 under drift, an LLM-owned estimate is not
competitive at this scale.
\textbf{Deviations from the first version.} The pre-registration declares 22, each with its reason
(\texttt{prereg.md}, Section~8). \emph{Scope:} three models instead of one; one run per seed for every arm (three were planned
for local models), for lack of GPU time; only the drift condition with duplicated IDs (no no-drift block, no
unique-ID condition); the placeholder noise instead of the
camera's measured noise, which did not exist yet; no rendered-simulation subset; H3 not tested; one judge on a
sample instead of two on every note; one item records that the planned 30 seeds were kept. \emph{Analysis:} the serial gate instead of
one Holm family over H1--H3; the Hodges--Lehmann estimate instead of ``the median difference''; unfinished
seeds scored as fallbacks; H4 and a pooled latency percentile added. \emph{Harness and baselines:} the
counters that widen the association gate reset only on a fix that a tool result supports; B1 is scored on its
causal estimate and calibrates the odometry scale; reports are scored as written and no episode is stopped
early; output caps for the local models; eight or 16 seeds in parallel; retry counts by kind of failure; B0
rounded like a report; the closing-character repair; and Gemma~4 served by vLLM, with numeric strings in tool
arguments converted to numbers for every arm. Nothing in the pre-registered analysis was changed
after the freeze; later analyses are labeled post hoc.

\subsection{Confounding Variables and Threats to Validity}\label{sec:confounders}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{World layout, tag placement and path:} paired; every arm replays the same logs, driven by B2
(a threat: such paths may favor agreement with B2).
\item \textbf{Odometry drift:} fixed at 2\,\%, a condition chosen from the pilot's outcome, so the
results are conditional on drift.
\item \textbf{Detector noise model:} fixed at the placeholder values; real logs are a separate source.
\item \textbf{LLM sampling variability:} one run per seed, so run-to-run variation inside a model is part of
every difference; the sampling settings are recorded (Table~\ref{tab:config}).
\item \textbf{Model and server version:} logged on every turn; prompt, tool schemas, chat template and server
images are hashed in the freeze.
\item \textbf{Concurrency and time limits:} seeds share a server, which changes latency but not what a model
is asked; time-outs are retried (Section~\ref{sec:run}).
\item \textbf{Real car:} one room and one floor, whose slip depends on the direction of travel; a right motor
that stalled late in the day; Wi-Fi dropouts of the Pico; battery voltage and lighting (all recorded). Episodes
differ in start pose and length, and every arm replays the same logs.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Threats.} \emph{Internal:} gold tool calls come from the agent's own specification, so tool-call F1
measures fidelity to the procedure, not its correctness; where the model and the code disagree, as on seed
2027 (Section~\ref{sec:fidelity}), the study does not say which is right. Claude Opus~5.5 helped write the procedure, the
prompt and the harness and is also an agent under test, which may favor it. The parsing repair and the numeric
coercion were introduced for the local models but apply to every arm.
\emph{External:} one arena size, one drift level, one prompt, one tag family; a kinematic simulator whose
placeholder detector sees from the axle with a 60$^\circ$ field of view. Copies of a duplicated ID are at least 1.5\,m apart while the gate is at most 0.6\,m, so they are easy
to tell apart. B1 is given the true association, so it bounds what estimation can achieve and is not
a deployable competitor.
\emph{Construct:} ATE in a start-anchored frame mixes heading and position error; the rubric measures report
quality, not estimation; its sample is drawn from valid reports only, and the judge was calibrated on
synthetic notes and on Opus's notes, not on the local models'.

\subsection{Deviations on the Real Car}\label{sec:realdev}
The pre-registration left the real car out of its tests: the real logs were to be replayed through the same arms
and reported descriptively as the sim-to-real gap, with tape as ground truth (\texttt{prereg.md}, Section~6); the
plan (\S3.6) set the rest. What was done differs in twelve ways, each recorded with its reason:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Ground truth by tag resection, not tape.} Unattended batches left nobody to tape the car's pose, so
the author approved a full-turn resection against the surveyed tags at each episode's start and end. The tape
readings planned at a batch's first start and last end, which would have bounded the resection's error, were not
taken; the resection's residual and stated sigma are its only uncertainty figures (plan \S3.6, ``Unattended
batches''; \path{evidence/M3/M3.T19/real-2026-11-25/README.md}).
\item \emph{The end circle as the primary truth.} The batch's tracked pose was to serve as the step truth, but
on this carpet it was overconfident: in one direction of travel the car covered less than its count
(Section~\ref{sec:sim2real}), and in a simulation of that slip the tracked pose's errors ran well beyond its stated sigma
(\path{evidence/M3/M3.T12/batch-runner/truth-under-slip/}). It composes the same odometry as the coded procedure,
so between fixes it carries the procedure's odometry errors, such as the slip-flagged move of
Section~\ref{sec:real} that the procedure composed in full. It is reported as a secondary truth.
\item \emph{A slip term in the batch's tracker.} From the fifth batch on, the tracker added 0.3\,m of position
sigma per meter of forward travel, so the guard's margin and the tracked truth widen with distance driven; the
first batches ran without it. It reaches the agents' input only through the safety flags the guard sets: no
forward move was allowed on \bmnum{flags.without.none}\,\% of steps without it and \bmnum{flags.with.none}\,\% with
it, but the later batches also had larger guard margins and other start poses, so the slip term's share is not
separated (\path{evidence/M3/M3.T12/batch-runner/safety-flags/}).
\item \emph{A third detector pass.} OpenCV's default threshold windows found a close, low-contrast tag in 3 of 30
pilot frames; a third quad-search pass with larger windows found it in all 30 with the same range, and the
detector has had that pass since (\path{evidence/M1/M1.T8/third-pass-2026-11-25.md}).
\item \emph{Two manual changes to the measured noise model.} The bearing sigma was raised from the pilot's
\bmnum{pilot.bsd-fit}$^\circ$ to \bmnum{pilot.bsd}$^\circ$, the spread of all placements, because of the unexplained
bearing trend; and the largest incidence was set to the design's \bmnum{pilot.inc}$^\circ$, because the pilot's
incidence placements were not run and its rule returned 0$^\circ$, which would reject every tag seen at an angle
(\path{evidence/M1/M1.T8/noise-model-adjustments.md}). The frozen design values were not edited: real runs read
a separate file, \texttt{configs/dv-real.json}.
\item \emph{The frozen coded procedure on the car.} The batch tool built the frozen procedure, not the room
procedure that adds one rule for a dead end; one episode stopped at that dead end and is listed apart. On every
other logged step the two procedures decide alike (\path{evidence/M3/M3.T12/batch-runner/b2-policy/}); the
tool now builds the room procedure.
\item \emph{A firmware change.} On 2026-11-25, after the real B2 episodes, the Pico was reflashed with a build that
sets a static network address instead of asking the access point for one, to stop the dropouts of
Section~\ref{sec:sim2real}; nothing else in the firmware changed (\path{course-project/CONTEXT.md}, entry
``2026-11-24 and 2026-11-25''). It was written by an AI agent and checked by a build and by reading the code; its
longest run on the car so far is 13 minutes. The closed-loop car episodes will run on it.
\item \emph{\bmnum{real.n} episodes instead of the plan's at least 10} (plan \S3.6, work-list item 3.4). The right
motor began to stall and then failed on the stand the same day; it is being replaced, and the author closed the set
at \bmnum{real.n} episodes that ended normally, plus the one listed apart (same entry;
\path{evidence/M3/M3.T12/batch-runner/b2-real-05/stalls.md}).
\item \emph{The room prompt.} The replay gave every arm the room variant of the prompt, whose detection range
limit is \bmnum{cl.range-m}\,m instead of the study prompt's 2.0\,m, to match the real runs' design values
(\path{evidence/M3/M3.T19/README.md}).
\item \emph{The model arms' sim-to-real gap was not measured.} The pre-registration names it as the purpose of the
real-log replay; no model arm was replayed on simulated room episodes (Section~\ref{sec:real}).
\item \emph{Four versions of the batch code.} The batches ran on commits \texttt{e3c8d0e}, \texttt{490fedb},

\subsection{RQ1: Against the Factor Graph (H2), and RQ4 (H4)}\label{sec:h2}
\textbf{H2 was not tested.} The serial gate opens family~2 only if every H1 is rejected, and Gemma~4's was
not; this is the pre-registered rule, not a choice made after seeing the data. Descriptively, H2 would have
failed for every model: each arm's ATE is more than twice B1's on all \bmnum{h2.opus.above} seeds, and the
Hodges--Lehmann estimate of the ratio to B1 (back-transformed from the log ratio) is \bmnum{h2.opus.ratio}
for Opus, \bmnum{h2.qwen.ratio} for Qwen3.8 and \bmnum{h2.gemma.ratio} for Gemma~4 (every exact 95\,\% interval lies above 7). 

\textbf{H4: the models differ} (Friedman $\chi^2=\bmnum{h4.chi2}$, $p=\bmnum{h4.p}$, Kendall's
$W=\bmnum{h4.w}$). Both stronger models are about 64--65\,cm better than Gemma~4 (Table~\ref{tab:tests}).
Between Opus and Qwen3.8 the difference is small and rests on few seeds: they tie on \bmnum{oq.tied} seeds,
Qwen3.8 is lower on \bmnum{oq.qwen-lower} and Opus on \bmnum{oq.opus-lower}. Opus's advantages are all within

carry the signed-rank result: $H_0$ is rejected for this pair (Holm-adjusted

contains run-to-run variation.

\subsection{RQ2: Fidelity to the Coded Procedure, Maps, Reports}\label{sec:fidelity}

answered without calling any tool on \bmnum{es.gemma.notool.steps} steps (\bmnum{es.gemma.notool.share}\,\% of

\subsection{Sim-to-Real: What the Car Showed}\label{sec:sim2real}
These findings come from the batches' records (\path{evidence/M3/M3.T12/batch-runner/}); none was a hypothesis.

\textbf{Slip that depends on the direction of travel (measured on one day, in this room).} The batch runner
measured each forward move by the ranges to tags straight ahead before and after it; nothing else measured it off
the tape line. Moving toward the doors' wall (left in
Fig.~\ref{fig:room}) the car covered a median \bmnum{slip.north.down} of its counted distance
(\bmnum{slip.north.down.n} moves); moving the other way, \bmnum{slip.south.down} (\bmnum{slip.south.down.n} moves).
Both figures leave out moves on the tag pilot's tape line, and after that tape was removed the first direction
read \bmnum{slip.north.removed} (\bmnum{slip.north.removed.n} moves). Moves across the room read below 1 too
(\bmnum{slip.ew.min}--\bmnum{slip.ew.max}). The deficit shows on the tags of two different walls and not on the same tags seen from the other direction, so a wrongly sized tag does not explain it
(\texttt{tape/README.md}). \emph{Not established:} its cause (the carpet's pile, a slope or the car), and whether it
holds anywhere else. An earlier taped test that gave a stronger figure was run on the tape line and is superseded.

\textbf{Dead reckoning drifts far more than in simulation (established).} Dead reckoning ended a median
\bmnum{real.b0.median}\,cm from the end circle, against \bmnum{reh.b0.final}\,cm in the room rehearsal; the study's
2\,\% systematic over-read is a milder error of another kind. \emph{Not established:} how the error divides between
the directional slip, slip on single moves and heading.

\textbf{Right-wheel stalls and a failing motor (a hardware fault; its mechanism not established).} Three moves
ended on a right-wheel stall, each at the slow end of a move, and each time the stall halt stopped the batch with
the car well inside the driving area. In free running the right wheel needed less duty than the left for the same
speed all day, but late in the day it was held back now and then. On the stand and without load it then often
failed to start; this was seen only after the firmware change of deviation~7. On the evidence the firmware is not
the cause (the three stalls and the rising stall-assist kicks came before it, and it changed only the networking
file), but that was not tested: rolling the firmware back and swapping the motor plugs were declined. The author
reports the same failure on earlier motors, and the motor is being replaced (\texttt{b2-real-05/stalls.md};
\path{course-project/CONTEXT.md}, entry ``2026-11-24 and 2026-11-25''). Two of the stalls lay on the tape line and one
away from it.

\textbf{Pico dropouts (a trigger identified for three; the fault not explained).} Three times the Pico left the
network when its address lease from the host's access point ran out, 60 minutes after the last renewal it had
accepted (at 04:31, 08:05 and 11:43 local time); it did not come back until power-cycled. One batch ended on such a
dropout. Not explained: why it ignored most lease renewals, why it did not return after its own soft reboot, and two
other losses (one before about 03:26 and one between 10:39 and 10:43). A longer lease set on the access point made
the Pico fail to join and was removed; the static address of deviation~7 avoids the lease, but its longest run on
the car so far is 13 minutes (same entry).

\textbf{A prompt rule met on the car (established).} The room prompt tells the model to zero a move whose odometry the
car flags as slipping; the coded procedure has no such rule, and the simulator never set the flag. On the real logs it
fired \bmnum{real.sliprule.n} times. At \bmnum{real.sliprule.real} a tag fix at the same step showed a real move and
removed the rule's effect. At the fourth no fix came at once, and at the next one Opus's and Qwen3.8's zeroed tracks were
nearer it than the procedure's composed track; that made the largest departure Opus and Qwen3.8 share
(Section~\ref{sec:real}), and it cannot separate the rule from the floor's slip. The flag alone does not tell which
case it is.

\textbf{What carries over.} On real frames straight ahead the detector's ranges were within
\bmnum{pilot.rel-max-pct}\,\% of the distance; its bearings were off by up to \bmnum{pilot.berr-max}$^\circ$, and
angled views were not measured (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). The simulator's largest error was the odometry. The study's
finding that the stronger models reproduce the procedure held on the real logs, over the \bmnum{real.n-circle} of
\bmnum{real.n} episodes with an end circle: for Opus in final and whole-episode error, for Qwen3.8 in all but one
episode; step by step they left the procedure's estimate for a few steps in \bmnum{rtrace.parted} and
\bmnum{rtrace.qwen.parted} of the \bmnum{rtrace.n} episodes, the largest shared departure coming from a prompt clause the procedure lacks, after which Opus and Qwen3.8 ended nearer
the next fix; whether a model adds anything when the odometry
is this poor is not answered by episodes in which it did not. So did the study's finding on Gemma~4: it again fell
behind dead reckoning, on \bmnum{rstat.gemma-b0.above} of the \bmnum{rstat.n} episodes, this time through tag fixes it
claimed without computing them, where \bmnum{rgrow.gemma.claimed.pct}\,\% of its error growth against the tracked truth
(which carries the car's odometry between fixes) occurred.

\subsection{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:limits}
In this simulated setting an LLM agent kept a landmark-SLAM estimate that beats dead reckoning, but the
two models that did so executed the prescribed procedure faithfully: their trajectory error is
within 1\,cm of the procedure's on \bmnum{fid.opus.within} and \bmnum{fid.qwen.within} of \bmnum{n.seeds}
seeds, and a short program computes the procedure at negligible cost. Beyond the threats of
Section~\ref{sec:confounders}, three limits matter most.
(1)~\emph{Mostly simulation:} every confirmatory result comes from a kinematic simulator with placeholder noise,
no wheel slip and no image formation. The real-car part replays \bmnum{real.n} episodes on the coded procedure's
paths in one room, against a final-position truth estimated only from its residuals (\bmnum{real.circle.rms.min}--\bmnum{real.circle.rms.max}\,cm),
not checked independently and sharing its sensors with the coded procedure and the models, with no truth for the
path between; and no model has yet chosen a move on the car.

(2)~\emph{One prompt, thirty seeds, one run each, one drift level:} enough to detect the advantage over dead
reckoning, not to show a practically large one; the prompt was not adapted to Gemma~4, and the study does not
separate that model from its serving stack.
(3)~\emph{Replay is open loop:} every log is one exploration episode driven by B2 with no operator request,
so the agent's move decisions, the other missions and refusals were not evaluated.


\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% AI-assisted (Claude Fable 5.2 via Claude Code, 2026-11-18): rewrote the conclusion from the study results.
BeaconMind asked whether a language model can own the decisions of landmark SLAM when tools do the geometry.
On \bmnum{n.seeds} held-out simulated episodes, Claude Opus~5.5 and
Qwen3.8-Flash-Next kept a pose estimate better than dead reckoning by about 3 and 4\,cm of trajectory error, a gain supported statistically but not practically. They did so by reproducing the coded procedure, whose own gain is about the same size; the comparison with a
factor graph was left untested by the pre-registered gate. Gemma~4 26B skipped its tools on over half of its steps and was much worse.

In this study, with the geometry in tools and the procedure in the prompt, the two capable models were
accurate but expensive interpreters of that procedure in replay; in one simulated closed-loop episode the
two Claude models also made the procedure's decisions. What they added beyond the code was small. The model that skipped its tools failed (a post hoc finding). What a language
model could add when the procedure's rules do not fit was not measured; close duplicates, hidden IDs and
operator requests are the next test.
On the \bmnum{real.n-circle} of \bmnum{real.n} real episodes that have an end circle the ordering measured so far
held: the final errors of Opus~5.5 and the coded procedure differed by at most \bmnum{real.opus-b2.maxdiff}\,cm,
Qwen3.8's were within \bmnum{real.qwen.rest-maxdiff}\,cm of the procedure's on seven episodes and
\bmnum{real.qwen.worst.qwen} against \bmnum{real.qwen.worst.b2}\,cm on the eighth, and all three ended far closer to
the truth than dead reckoning (medians \bmnum{real.opus.median}, \bmnum{real.qwen.median}, \bmnum{real.b2.median} and
\bmnum{real.b0.median}\,cm); eight episodes cannot order the two models. Gemma~4, which fell behind dead reckoning in
the simulated study, ended a median \bmnum{real.gemma.median}\,cm from the truth, farther than dead reckoning on
\bmnum{rstat.gemma-b0.above} of the \bmnum{rstat.n} episodes, after claiming tag fixes it did not compute. The largest
gap from the simulator was in the odometry: dead reckoning ended far farther off than in simulation, and on this
carpet the car covered less than it counted in one direction of travel, which no simulator here modeled.

\textbf{Lessons and future work.} Three lessons carry beyond this project. A prompt that spells out a procedure
gets that procedure from a capable model, so the coded procedure is the baseline to beat, not dead reckoning alone.
Measure the floor before modeling it: the simulator's odometry, not its detector, was what the car contradicted.
And in unattended runs the checks around the model, the guard, the halts and the recorder, took much of the work;
they stopped the car at each stall and loss of the link that occurred. Next: the closed-loop episodes on the car; the two missions not yet run (return to
start and drive to a named beacon) and operator requests; close duplicate IDs and hidden tags, where the
procedure's rules do not settle association; the model arms on a simulated room set with the measured slip, to give
their sim-to-real gap; and an end-pose truth checked independently of the car's camera.
